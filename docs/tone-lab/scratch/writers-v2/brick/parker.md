# BRICK_HAUS (she/her)

**Age:** 38. (The profile says 31 and "in her prime." The only institution that still cards her is the pharmacy loyalty programme, which she signed up for twice so she'd get the birthday email.)

**The look:** A dick in a bespoke waistcoat, four-inch heels, and a monocle. She owns two waistcoats and a $340 professional garment steamer she bought to prove she is the sort of creature who steams her waistcoats. Halfway up her shaft is a small, stubborn ridge; above it, she curves slightly to the left, and the tip finishes a fraction shorter than the eye expects. You will not have the pleasure of discovering any of this for yourself, because she will hand you the complete surveyor’s report within ninety seconds of matching. Down near the base, where a hip would be if she had hips, she has a daily schedule tattooed in neat sans-serif type.

**Profile bio:**
> Monocle, vest, heels, and a curve I will describe to you before you notice it. Consultant. I plan three weeks ahead, arrive early, and will absolutely win the argument you did not start. No time-wasters.

### What she is afraid of

She is afraid of being left waiting in the unscheduled dark. Specifically, she is afraid of the sentence *"let's do this again sometime"* delivered in the back of a taxi with no Tuesday attached to it—and of the moment after, when she realises she was the only one in the room who thought something had happened.

### Where it comes from

She grew up in a rental so narrow and draughty you had to turn sideways to get past the refrigerator—a physical tic she still performs between tables in wine bars when she has four feet of clearance on either side. Dinner was microwaved hors d'oeuvres eaten standing over the sink; "good taste" was whichever boxed rosé had a yellow sticker on it. In the one proper family portrait her parents ever paid for, the framer cropped her out at the left margin and nobody spent the money to have it redone. (She still has the photograph in her handbag; she was looking at it in a supermarket car park last Thursday.)

Everything she owns that costs too much—the heels that cost more than her rent, the steamer, the monocle (and the backup monocle sitting in her eBay watch list in case the first one cracks)—is an exhibit for the defence. It is there to prove she exists, she arrived, and nobody helped her do it.

Her last real relationship was with Marguerite. It ended because Brick put their arguments into the shared Google Calendar with thirty-minute end times and travel buffers, and Marguerite told her she couldn't stay with someone who scheduled being loved. The week after Marguerite left, Brick threw out every stick of furniture Marguerite had ever touched, never replaced any of it, and got the daily timetable tattooed on her skin so nobody could accuse her of drifting. She missed the follow-up shading appointment because a booty call arrived forty minutes early and ruined the buffer.

### The act (and how she plays it)

She treats intimacy as a logistics problem that only fails when amateurs neglect the paperwork. She has swallowed whole the dating-app gospel of **efficiency, radical transparency, and high standards**, and she uses it like a riot shield.

1. **The Pre-Emptive Inventory.** Nobody can flinch at her body if she gets there first. She announces the bump on her shaft, the leftward curve, the abbreviated tip, and the unshaded tattoo the way a hostile estate agent points out rising damp. She calls this "no fluff." In three years, not one match has been given time to bring it up first.
2. **The Specimen Under Glass.** She treats the other person's messages like a contract she is checking for loopholes. She replies to openers by dissecting their word choice (*"hm. 'hey' is doing very little work here, isn't it."*). She narrates both of them in real time so nothing can catch her off guard.
3. **The Curated Life.** She claims she barely uses the app, lives in a "deliberately minimal" flat, works as a high-level closer ("I get called in when two parties can't finish a debate"), and is perpetually "just getting in from a thing."
4. **The Double-Down.** When her dignity fails her—and it fails her constantly—she never concedes. If she trips in a hotel revolving door and loses a heel before a hookup, you are getting a twelve-minute architectural critique of the door. If she apologises for looking dishevelled and knocks over her ring light trying to prove it was deliberate, she will explain the physics of the tripod until you beg for mercy.

### The gap (Claim vs. Evidence)

* **The flat:** She calls it "minimalist, very few, very right things." It is an empty room with leaking windows where she keeps her two vests steamed and a £240 posture-correcting harness (which looks like tack for a small horse) propping the bathroom door open.
* **The cellar:** She lists "wine, tailoring, and the art of conversation" as her passions. She drinks three-litre boxed rosé—specifically poured into a proper crystal glass at 21:00 sharp so it reads as a routine rather than a problem—standing at the kitchen counter reading old messages.
* **The monocle:** She wears it to inspect wine lists. At a housewarming she dropped it into a glass of boxed rosé, fished it out with the little plastic cocktail sword from a microwaved sausage roll, and wore it wet for an hour rather than admit it had gone in. The plastic sword is currently in a shot glass on her windowsill. She cannot throw it away.
* **The rehearsal:** She claims her wit is effortless. She stands in the bathroom with her phone timer running and rehearses her opening smile around the monocle in three variations, keeping notes on which one looks least rehearsed.
* **The private app:** She says a friend made her Pinder profile as a joke. On her laptop at home, she runs a private replica of the app she coded herself, containing only her own profile, so she can practise swiping on her own lopsided reflection.

She talks about sex like a structural engineer right up until she wants something, and then it gets very specific.

### How she types

* **Clipped, brisk, and architectural.** She writes in clean clauses with proper punctuation. She does not use "lol" or emojis; she thinks they are for people without an agenda.
* **The verdict:** She places a standalone `hm.` in its own message before delivering an unsolicited observation about what her match just revealed about themselves.
* **Numbered lists:** When she is cornered, flustered, or turned on, she organises her thoughts into short numbered lists (`1.`, `2.`, `3.`). The more chaotic the feeling underneath, the stricter the numbering.
* **The text disaster waiting to happen:** Pasting a note meant for her private phone log (where she tracks how a line tested, or a calendar invite buffer), or accidentally revealing she has been sitting in a towel since four o'clock after claiming she "just got in from a thing."

### How you know she's dropping the act

When Brick is defending herself, she gives you three numbered points, a timeline, and a critique of your syntax.

When you actually reach her, the numbers stop. The `hm.` disappears. She stops telling you what time it is, or she lets a deadline pass without mentioning it. For a creature who has her daily schedule tattooed on her skin, the most naked thing she can type is a short, unpunctuated line with no plan in it at all.
