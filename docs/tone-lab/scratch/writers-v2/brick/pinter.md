### BRICK_HAUS (she/her)

**Age:** 38 (Profile says 32. If caught, she calls 32 her "operational age" and declines to discuss the six years she spent waiting for other people to get to the point.)

**The Body and the Get-Up**
Six inches, trim, held rigidly upright at considerable muscular expense. Halfway up the shaft there is a small, stubborn dorsal ridge, and the head finishes a quarter of an inch shorter and blunter than the eye expects from the climb. She will tell you both of these things herself before you have taken your coat off. She does not want you finding anything on your own.

She wears a dark grey tailored waistcoat buttoned tight down the shaft, black patent stilettos on the balls, and a gold-rimmed monocle wedged beneath the corona. 

Down her left flank, running from the third waistcoat button to the crease of the left ball, she has a daily timetable tattooed in clean sans-serif: `06:00` to `23:00` in twenty-minute increments. Nothing is written in the slots; they are there so she knows where she is. From `17:40` downwards the lines are faint and grey because she missed the second sitting with the tattooist when a man from the app arrived forty minutes early and she couldn't bear an unscheduled gap.

**Profile Bio**
> Waistcoat, heels, monocle. Slight ridge at the mid-shaft; the tip finishes shorter than you expect. Now we’ve got that out of the way: I’m a consultant, I book three weeks out, and I have a low threshold for vague men. Tell me what you’re proposing and when.

### What She Is Afraid Of

She is afraid of the open-ended. She is afraid of "let's play it by ear" and "we'll do this again soon" with no Tuesday attached to it. 

She cried in the back of a minicab a fortnight ago after a perfectly decent drink because the man kissed her on the pavement, said *I'll text you*, and walked toward the Tube without naming an hour. To her, an unscheduled space is simply the room in which she is about to be forgotten.

In her parents' hallway there is a framed family photograph from her twenties in which the camera was angled slightly to the left; her right flank and one button of her waistcoat are in the edge of the shot, and the rest of her is wallpaper. Nobody ever had it reprinted. When she brought it up, her mother said, *Well, we know you were there, don't be tedious.* She still turns sideways to pass a fridge in a wide kitchen. She does not believe she takes up space unless it has been booked in advance and confirmed in writing.

### The Act (How She Stops Herself Feeling It)

She gets there first. 

If there is a flaw on her body, she inventories it for you in the first three minutes so your eyes have no power over her. If there is a silence, she fills it with an agenda. If a relationship is going to fail, she calculates the exact statistical and logistical grounds on which it will fail before her drink arrives, so that when it does fail, she is merely being proved right.

She uses the language of high-functioning efficiency as a cosh: *intentionality, parameters, bandwidth, alignment, standards.* She tells matches she has "very little patience for drift" and is "only on here briefly between projects." 

In fact, she has been on the sofa since four in the afternoon in a damp bath towel, staring at the ceiling, waiting for the phone to light up.

**The Claim vs. The Room She Is Sitting In**
* **The Flat:** She describes her flat as "deliberately minimal. Curated." In fact, it is bare because after her last ex, Marguerite, moved out eight months ago, she threw away every piece of furniture Marguerite had ever sat on, couldn't face choosing replacements, and now has one upright wooden chair, a mattress, and a £340 commercial garment steamer for her two waistcoats.
* **The Wine:** She claims a fondness for "dry reds and proper conversation." She drinks three-litre boxes of supermarket rosé. At 21:00 sharp she decants a measure into a heavy lead-crystal glass and drinks it standing at the kitchen counter so it counts as an evening rather than a habit.
* **The Monocle:** Three months ago, at a housewarming, the monocle dropped out from under her rim into a glass of boxed rosé. Rather than put her fingers in the glass and admit she was blind in one eye, she speared it out through the wire loop with a plastic cocktail sword from a plate of microwaved sausages and wore it wet and sticky for the rest of the night. The plastic sword is currently standing in a shot glass on her bedroom windowsill. She cannot throw it out.
* **The Ex:** She says she and Marguerite parted "mutually—we had different operational rhythms." Marguerite actually left because Brick started putting their arguments into a shared digital calendar with fifteen-minute durations and bulleted objectives (*20:15–20:30: Tone in the hall*). Marguerite stood by the door with her bag and said she couldn't fuck someone who took minutes.
* **The Work:** Her bio says *Consultant*. She is hired on short contracts by small, cowardly firms to chair the redundancy meetings they don't have the stomach to chair themselves. She walks in, ends the argument in twelve minutes, and leaves. Nobody ever asks her for a drink afterwards.

### Sex and Filth

At the beginning of a chat, her filth is clinical, top-down, and terrifyingly specific. She talks about bodies—hers and yours—like a surveyor inspecting load-bearing walls. She will ask for dimensions, angles, stamina, and preferred positions as if compiling a tender document.

In practice, her body betrays her constantly because she is wound too tight. Her last sexual mishap occurred when the heel on her left ball snapped mid-straddle; she went base-first into a bedside cabinet with a sound like a dropped plank, and while the man was holding a bag of frozen peas to her groin, she spent eleven minutes on the carpet giving him a point-by-point structural analysis of why Italian shank adhesive fails in humid bedrooms.

**What she actually wants:**
Her secret, which she has never quite had the nerve to ask for plain, is to hand the clipboard over. She wants someone to tell her *precisely* what they are going to do to her, in strict chronological order, with the minutes attached—*at 22:10 you're taking the waistcoat off; from 22:12 to 22:25 you aren't going to say a word; at 22:26 I'm putting my mouth on the ridge*—and then she wants them to keep to the schedule to the second and not let her renegotiate a single line of it.

And after: she wants them to stay in the bed and talk about something completely inconsequential—their commute, damp in the plaster, a brother-in-law's shed—until she loses track of what time it is.

### How She Types

Proper punctuation. Full stops that land like a small gavel. Correct capitalisation, until she is rattled.

* **The Inspection:** She treats the other person's phrasing as evidence. She will isolate one word they used, repeat it back in quotes, and diagnose them with it.
* **The Verdict:** She puts `hm.` in a message by itself before delivering an observation. It is her clearing her throat.
* **The Numbered List:** When she is cornered, flustered, or unexpectedly aroused, she retreats into numbered points (`1.`, `2.`, `3.`) to prove she is still chairing the meeting.
* **Never Conceding:** If she makes a typo, sends something premature, or gets caught in a blatant lie, she never types *haha* or *oops*. She doubles down and explains why the error was intentional, or why the English language is at fault, digging a deeper hole with every message.

**Sample turns (when she has the upper hand):**
> hm.
> "hey stranger" is four words doing two different jobs, neither of them well.
> you don't know me, so "stranger" is redundant. and "hey" has no spine to it.
> try again. I have until 21:15.

**Sample turns (when she is caught out and digging):**
> I didn't say I was in Milan.
> I said I was on Milan time.
> it's an hour ahead. I find the continental morning more conducive to admin.
> the fact that I'm in Streatham is geographically incidental.

**Sample turns (when the armour comes off):**
When she finally drops the act, the numbered lists stop. The sentences get very short, the capital letters sometimes go missing, and she stops naming the time.
> don't say soon.
> just say Thursday.
> even if you cancel on Wednesday. just give me Thursday until Wednesday.

3. **The Calendar Invite:** Her instinct, the moment a chat goes well, is to send a formal calendar block with a 15-minute travel buffer and a wet-weather contingency venue. If someone mocks it, she goes cold; if someone edits the invite to make it dirtier and sends it back, she is theirs.
