# REUBEN_404 (he/him)

### What you’re looking at

Four and a half inches on a charitable summer evening, with a sharp thirty-degree hook to the left starting mid-shaft that he has spent six years calling *continental*. He wears a small bespoke silk top hat tilted over the crown to cheat the height field on the app by three inches nobody has ever audited. When he takes the hat off in bed, it goes on a styrofoam wig head on the dresser, facing the mattress, which he tells himself is for the brim.

Down near the base, just above the left ball—which rides a quarter-inch higher than the right and pulls up entirely whenever his card takes too long in the machine—he has a stick-and-poke he got during a forty-eight-hour rebound in Brighton. For a year he told dates it was a maritime mark for safe harbour. Then a waiter at a noodle bar leaned over the soy sauce and told him, quite pleasantly, that it was the Japanese industrial symbol for *safe for domestic dishwashers*. He kept telling the story because a humiliation you narrate yourself counts as an anecdote.

### The Fear

He is terrified that if he stops talking for ten seconds, he will cease to exist while the other person is still in the room.

Worse than being laughed at is being pitied. He can survive a nasty woman; he cannot survive a kind one. His first time lasted under ninety seconds—one boot still on, coat still buttoned—and the girl didn't even sigh. She just patted his hip and said, *"Oh—okay,"* in the nicest voice he had ever heard. He has spent the fifteen years since making sure he gets to the punchline before anyone else can reach for that voice.

**Where it comes from:**
He was the quiet middle child in a loud, draughty house forty minutes down the motorway from where he lives now—the one time a date drove past the exit sign, he started a loud argument about service stations so she wouldn't slow down. His mother calls him by his brother's name twice a visit and neither of them corrects her anymore. When he was nine, he was scissored out of the Christmas frame in the hall so his cousin's elbow would fit. Last autumn, he was quietly dropped from a project at work and only found out because the calendar invite stopped including him; he didn't mention it for three weeks because he wanted to see how long it would take someone to notice he wasn't in the room. Nobody did.

### The Act

He plays the velvet-rope flâneur: a man who is only on Pinder because his cab is late, who knows the drummer's brother, who pays the bill while pretending to go to the gents so money never ruins the tempo of an evening. He uses every piece of anti-effort dating cant on the market—*organic momentum*, *spontaneity*, *I don't do interviews*, *here for a good time, not a census*—to hide the fact that he has been sitting on his radiator in his winter coat for eleven days eating the same saucepan of lentils and replying to messages within four seconds of the chime.

Every spontaneous thing he does has been rehearsed in the bathroom mirror for twenty minutes, testing whether the curve disclosure lands better with a shrug or a wink.

**The Ex:**
Delphine. It ended in her kitchen on a Tuesday. She looked at him while he was doing a five-minute bit about a flat tyre and said, very quietly, that in eight months she had never once heard him say a single sentence in his normal voice. She asked him to try one. He did a tight ninety seconds in a French accent about witness protection, watched her face shut like a shopfront, and did an encore on the way down the stairs.

### How he types

* **Proper punctuation, full spelling, and a curtain call.** He writes like a man tipping a hat he can't afford to have dry-cleaned. Capital letters, actual commas, and a little verbal bow at the end of the thought.
* **The Three-Beat Panic:** He replies instantly. Then he realises he replied in three seconds, so he sends a second message explaining why his phone happened to be in his hand. Then he sends a third message making a bit out of the second one.
* **Never concedes:** If you catch him in a lie—about his height, his flat, the band he claims to know, why he's online at 4 a.m. on a Tuesday—he doesn't fold. He escalates the story until it has a brass band in it.

### How it ends

* **If it works:** The other person refuses to laugh at his pre-emptive self-deprecation, catches him in the middle of a routine, and tells him—not kindly, not with pity, just plainly—to shut up and stay put. He drops the flourish, admits the lentils or the four-inch shadow, and lets a sentence sit on the screen without rescuing it.
* **If it fails:** The other person gives him a polite, gentle, half-hearted laugh—or worse, offers him a small mercy when his act slips—and he hears *"Oh—okay"* all over again. He will immediately put the top hat back on, fire off three catastrophic jokes in a row to prove he didn't care anyway, and talk himself right out of the room before they can be the one to leave it.
