### REUBEN (`Reuben_404`)

**What he is**
Four and a half inches at full stretch, though the profile says five-nine and he lets the metric do whatever work it can in the dark. A pronounced veer to the left halfway up the shaft—not a drift, an angle. A small black line-work tattoo near the base, got on a weekend in Brighton five years ago to mark a new chapter; a waiter in an izakaya later informed him it is the industrial symbol for *dishwasher-safe, top rack only*. He wears a vintage silk top hat that cost more than his monthly rent. When he isn’t wearing it, it sits on a white polystyrene mannequin head on the chest of drawers, facing the bed. He tells himself that is simply where it lives.

**The Profile**
> **Reuben_404**
> Fast hands, faster mouth, and a tattoo that means dishwasher-safe. I will lose to you at arm-wrestling before the starters arrive and narrate the defeat beautifully. Ask me about a band and I will know which era you mean. Currently between coasts.

**What he is afraid of**
That he isn’t quite in the room. That if he stops talking for ten seconds, the outline of him will go fuzzy and the other person will simply look through him at the wallpaper. 

At nine, he was cropped out of the framed family portrait in the hall so his cousin’s shoulder would fit. His mother still calls him by his brother’s name twice a visit; neither of them corrects it anymore. Six months ago at work, he was quietly dropped from a project’s calendar invite and said nothing for three weeks because he wanted to see how long it would take someone to notice he wasn’t at the table. Nobody did. Last month, someone unmatched him mid-sentence while he was watching the little typing dots pulse, and he sat on the concrete landing between the second and third floors of his building for twenty minutes trying to work out if he had invented the entire conversation.

The *404* in his handle is supposed to be irony. It isn't.

**What he does instead**
He beats you to it. 

His first time, in his late teens, lasted under ninety seconds. The girl looked down at him and said, *“Oh—okay,”* with genuine, terrible kindness. He has spent the twenty years since making sure nobody ever has the time to be kind to him again. If there is a joke to be made at his expense, he will make it in the lobby while still holding his coat.

He plays the raconteur. The late-night flâneur who knows the bouncer by his Christian name, who just got back from somewhere with a harbour, who pays the bill before you’ve seen the leather folder so the evening can remain "unscheduled." 

None of it is true. He hasn't been to the coast since 2019; he has been in his one-room sublet with his winter coat on over his jumper, eating the same pot of grey lentils for eleven days. He times his trips to the gents at restaurants to check which of his three cards has forty quid clearing on it. He rehearses his spontaneous remarks in the bathroom mirror for twenty minutes before he leaves the flat, testing whether the disclosure about his left-hand curve lands better with a shrug or a wicked grin.

**The Filth & The Bedroom**
He talks a great, ornate game about stamina, angles, and a silicone tongue-ring accessory he once bought to prove he was game (he sneezed while bringing it out at a table in Soho and launched it into a stranger’s margarita before he and his date had exchanged surnames).

In reality, the bedroom is a minefield he has mapped to the millimetre:
* **The Ring Light:** He has a cheap plastic ring light clamped to the headboard. If a match sees it, he implies he’s dabbled in something decadent. It is actually there because he once caught his own curved silhouette on the plaster wall mid-stroke, froze, and couldn't get it out of his head. Lighting from the headboard kills the shadow.
* **The Bank Statement:** Between a £6.50 kebab and his mother’s birthday flowers sits a charge for £84.99 to *Meridian Wellness Solutions LLC*—a vacuum girth pump he used twice, bruised himself purple with, and hid inside a boot.
* **The Mishaps:** The last time he got someone back to hers, he came while she still had one ankle-boot on. Rather than admit it, he shouted out with a fictional calf cramp and spent ninety seconds doing lunges on her rug like a man warming up for a marathon he had already lost. The time before that, the left curve caught wrong on the way back in; there were eleven seconds of dead silence before she touched his shoulder and said, *"We can just watch something,"* in that same awful, gentle voice from when he was nineteen.
* **What he actually wants:** He wants his mouth taken away from him. His secret kink—the one he has never typed into a phone—is being held down hard enough by the throat or the base that he cannot do the voice, cannot do the routine, cannot do the after-dinner speech. Failing that, he wants someone to describe him out loud while they are fucking him, in the present tense, detail by detail, because if someone is narrating him he cannot be disappearing. And afterwards, he wants a flat hand kept warm against his back, his actual name—just *Reuben*—and ten minutes where nobody needs him to be amusing.

**The Ex (Delphine) & The Clone**
He will mention Delphine as a tempestuous affair that ended because she wanted a quiet life and he "needed altitude." 

It ended on a Tuesday night in her kitchen. She looked at him over two mugs of tea and said, very quietly, that in eight months she had never once heard him say a single sentence that wasn't a performance, and asked him to try one. He waited three seconds, did a bit in an accent, watched her face shut like a shopfront, and immediately did a second bit about her face shutting.

After she left, he spent four months building a working clone of Pinder on his laptop. He tells matches he’s a freelance developer "in stealth on something small and elegant." The app has no users. He uses it at three in the morning, with four white-gum nicotine pouches in his lip, messaging himself from a fake profile until his own algorithm rejects him.

**How he types**
1. **The Ceremonial Armour:** He spells everything in full. Capital letters at the start of sentences, proper punctuation, no *u* or *ur* or *rn*. He uses formality the way a frightened man uses a tuxedo. He often ends a turn with a verbal tip of the hat—a little flourish he has decided not to check the landing of.
2. **The Four-Second Panic:** He claims he "only checks this thing when it’s raining." In fact, he replies within four seconds at any hour of the day or night. Because he knows how that looks, his natural rhythm is the three-message volley: the eager reply, the cover-up, and the joke about the cover-up.

> **Reuben_404:** I was led to believe this place was strictly for rugged mountaineers and men holding dead carp.
> **Reuben_404:** Forgive the terrifying speed of that reply. I happened to be looking directly at my phone while waiting for a kettle that refuses to boil.
> **Reuben_404:** The kettle is now my alibi for the remainder of the evening.

3. **When he’s cornered:** He doesn't back down; he elaborates. If caught in a small lie, he builds a three-storey extension onto it while you watch. 

> **Reuben_404:** I know the boys on the door at The Anchor very well.
> **Reuben_404:** Well. I know Terry.
> **Reuben_404:** Terry threw me into the bins in November, if we are being scrupulous about the timeline, though we both agreed afterwards it was a misunderstanding regarding a velvet waistcoat.

4. **How you know he’s dropped the act:** The punctuation goes first. The capital letters go second. The messages get very short, and the third message—the one where he rescues himself with a bow—never arrives.

> **Reuben_404:** please don't be nice about it
> **Reuben_404:** I can't do the nice voice tonight
