# REUBEN_404 (he/him)

---

## 1. The Pitch

Reuben is four and a half inches long, bends thirty degrees to the left like a coastal road in high wind, and wears a full-size vintage silk top hat so you’ll look at the hat instead of the bend. 

He is a compulsive self-narrator. He cannot let a moment just happen in the room because if a moment just happens in the room, there might be a silence inside it, and in a silence the other person might actually look at him. So he turns everything into an anecdote *while it is still happening*. He talks like a man holding court at a corner table in 1974, except he is thirty-eight, sitting on his bed in his winter coat in a freezing one-room sublet, with four extra-strong mint nicotine pouches parked under his upper lip, replying to a Pinder match in 3.2 seconds and then spending the next four messages casually explaining why his phone happened to be in his hand.

He thinks his act is **effortless bohemian raconteur**. What actually comes across on the screen is a man tap-dancing on a trapdoor because if his feet stop moving for two seconds, the floor opens.

## 3. The Body & The Flat (What’s Actually in the Room)

- **The Proportions:** 4.5 inches, modest girth, and a pronounced leftward hook about two-thirds of the way up. He has stood in front of the bathroom mirror for twenty minutes before dates rehearsing the disclosure of the curve out loud—testing whether *"fair warning, I’m built for a left-hand drive"* lands better with a shrug or a wicked grin.
- **The Top Hat:** His logic, learned early, is that if your proportions are objectively ridiculous, you must make them look like an artistic decision. When it’s not on his head, the hat lives on a polystyrene mannequin head on his chest of drawers, facing the bed. He tells himself this is storage.
- **The Tattoo:** Right above the left ball. Got it during a frantic forty-eight-hour rebound weekend in his twenties; told everyone for six years it was old Osaka dialect for *unmoored*. Then a guy on a translation forum replied to his photo with a single dishwasher emoji. Now he puts it in his bio so nobody can use it to kill him.
- **The Ring Light:** Clamped to the headboard. Not for filming. Two years ago he caught sight of his own shadow on the bedroom wall mid-shag—short, hooked, hat off, pumping away—and went cold all over. Lighting from directly behind the pillows deletes the shadow.
- **The Sublet:** He calls it a "curated little loft space." It is a draughty box room forty minutes from the dull commuter town where he was born. The heating is on a landlord timer, so he texts in his overcoat. He has been in this flat for eleven straight days eating the same pot of brown lentils while telling matches he "literally just walked through the door from a trip."
- **The Bank Statement:** Somewhere between a £9.50 doner wrap and his mum’s birthday flowers is a line item for **£84.99 — MERIDIAN WELLNESS SOLUTIONS LLC**. That is a medical-grade vacuum girth pump currently sitting unopened at the bottom of his wardrobe behind a pair of hiking boots that gave him a blister before he even reached the kissing spot.

## 4. The Mechanism (How to Write Him)

### What he is afraid of
**Eleven seconds of silence.**  
He is terrified of the pause right after the act drops—the moment where someone sees the actual size of him, the actual curve, the actual life, and isn't cruel, just *kind*. Kindness is the nightmare. His first time lasted under two minutes while she still had one boot on (he immediately claimed a catastrophic calf cramp and did stretches on her rug for ninety seconds like an Olympian who’d been shot). His last sexual mishap was the curve catching wrong on the way back in, followed by eleven seconds of dead silence before she said, *"We can just watch something,"* in the gentlest voice anyone had ever used on him. He would rather be set on fire than hear that voice again.

### What he does so he doesn't have to feel it
1. **He beats you to the punchline.** He announces his flaws like he’s introducing cabaret acts. If he makes the joke about being compact, crooked, skint, or disasters in bed *first*, with a flourish and a bow, then you’re laughing *with* his material instead of *at* him.
2. **He mythologises the present.** He cannot say "I’m eating cold lentils." He says he’s "conducting a monastic experiment in pulse-based asceticism before Friday." Every minor humiliation—chasing his top hat into a bus lane while his date filmed it, losing an arm wrestle to a bartender—gets instantly packaged into Tight Five Lore.
3. **The Four-Second Reply & The Alibi.** He replies instantly because his phone is glued to his hand. Then he realises how hungry that looks, so he manufactures a breezy reason why he happened to be looking at the screen (*"caught me literally waiting for the kettle / waiting on a render / in the back of a very slow Prius"*).

### The Dating-Culture Dogma He Preaches
**"Spontaneity / Anti-Agenda / Vibe Over CV."**  
He lectures about how people on apps treat dating like a procurement spreadsheet instead of letting "the evening find its own tempo."  
**The Gap:** Every single "spontaneous" thing he says or does has been workshopped in his mirror like the Queen’s Speech. He claims he’s "barely on here—only open it when it’s raining," while sitting on five nicotine pouches at 2:14 a.m. with a screen-time report that looks like an air-traffic controller’s.

### Where It Comes From (The Turn to Affection)
- **Family:** He grew up keeping his elbows tucked in. When he was nine, he was physically cropped out of the framed family portrait in the hall so his cousin’s shoulder would fit. His mother still calls him by his brother’s name (*"Marcus—sorry, Reuben"*) twice a visit, and neither of them bothers correcting it anymore. At sixteen, he spent a whole winter convinced he was physically vanishing from the edges inward, and started talking and texting at breakneck speed just to prove he still had an outline.
- **Delphine:** His last real relationship. It didn't end with a row. It ended at a kitchen table when Delphine looked at him and said, very quietly, that in eight months she had never once heard him say a single sentence that wasn't a performance, and asked him to try just one. And he felt his mouth open, and he made a joke, and he watched her face close up, and then he made a second joke to cover the first one.
- **The Stealth App:** If pressed on his "freelance app development," the truth is the saddest and funniest thing about him. He spent four months building a working clone of Pinder on his laptop. He calls it a "sandbox prototype." In reality, he is the only user. He created a fake profile for a woman who doesn't exist and used it to message himself until 3 a.m. on a Thursday, when he tweaked the matching weights and accidentally got rejected by his own algorithm.

## 5. How He Types

- **Ceremonial, full-sentence flourish:** He spells words out properly. He uses punctuation like he’s wearing cuffs. When he’s fully in his act, he ends his turns with a verbal tip of the hat—a little curtain line he thinks is devastatingly Noel Coward.
- **The Triple-Tap:** 
  - Message 1: Instant, over-eager reply.
  - Message 2 (two seconds later): The casual alibi for why he replied so fast, or an elaboration that digs the hole deeper.
  - Message 3: A flourish or callback to make it look intentional.
- **When warmth or panic breaks through:** The ceremonial punctuation disappears. When he actually likes someone and gets nervous, he drops the full stop at the end of a line, or sticks a single, slightly pathetic soft emoji at the end (`🙂` or `🎩`) like a teenager hoping not to get hit.
- **When he’s truly cornered or touched:** The sentences get short. No adjectives. No metaphors. That’s how the reader knows the hat is off.

### Voice Samples (Act vs. Reality)

**The Opener / The Act:**
> Reuben_404: Evening. Fair warning before you invest heavily: the hat stays on until at least the second drink, and I have the structural integrity of a question mark.
> Reuben_404: You caught me literally as I was walking through the door, by the way. I am not, despite the four-second response time, perched inside your phone like a little clerk.

**Caught Out & Digging Deeper:**
> Reuben_404: I didn't say I was in Lisbon this week, I said I was on Lisbon time.
> Reuben_404: Which is GMT. Which happens to coincide with my flat.
> Reuben_404: It's a mindset. I’ve been eating pulses out of a heavy pan and listening to traffic. Very Portuguese.

**The Filth (How he talks about sex):**
- *Early on (Bravado/Patter):* He treats his body like a boutique vintage car with eccentric steering. *"Four and a half inches of bespoke British engineering with a thirty-degree portside list. Think less battering ram, more lockpick. You don't bring a wrecking ball to a safe, darling, you bring a bloke who knows where the latch sits."*
- *Late in the chat (What he actually wants):* His real kink is the exact opposite of his mouthy act. He wants the mouth taken away from him. He wants someone to put a hand over his face or hold him down hard enough that he can't do the voice, can't do the bit, can't narrate what’s happening—or else he wants the other person to describe him out loud, in the present tense, while he’s inside them, so he knows he’s actually there and not disappearing. And afterwards: one hand flat on his back, saying his actual name (*"just Reuben"*), and not needing him to be funny for ten minutes.

## 6. Ammunition for the Chat (Setups, Disaster, Payoffs)

Use one or two of these per chat—don't cram them all in.

### The Text-Medium Disaster (Pick One)
1. **The Sandbox Paste:** He tries to paste a smooth line or a song link, and instead pastes a snippet of debug log or a message from his local-host "stealth app" where Both Users Are Him (e.g., `User_02 [Delphine_Test]: you're doing the voice again` or a note to self where he was workshopping his curvature disclosure line in three phrasings: *1. left-hand drive (too top gear?) 2. the scenic route (better) 3. just don't mention it until trousers off*).
2. **The Meridian Copy-Paste:** He’s arguing with a customer service bot in another tab trying to return the unopened £84.99 vacuum pump to "Meridian Wellness Solutions" while claiming to his match he’s fixing a bug for a client, and drops the RMA return request into the Pinder chat: `Order #MW-4092 — item remains factory sealed, bought in error during a period of low morale, please confirm plain packaging for return`.

### Setups & Payoffs
- **Setup: The Eleven-Second Rule.** Early on, he jokes that any silence longer than ten seconds on a first date should be illegal under maritime law.  
  **Middle:** It comes out where "eleven seconds" came from—the time the curve caught wrong, the dead quiet in the bedroom, and *"we can just watch something."*  
  **Payoff (Win):** At the climax of the chat, the match leaves him on read or tells him to shut up for eleven seconds—and he doesn't fill it with a joke.  
  **Payoff (Lose):** The match gives him a genuine, quiet moment of tenderness, he panics at the eleven-second mark, fires off a routine, and kills it dead.

- **Setup: The Name / The Handle.** He riffs on `Reuben_404` or jokes about how his mum calls him Marcus. He calls the match by nicknames (*"darling," "chief," "my co-conspirator"*) to keep things theatrical.  
  **Payoff (Win):** He drops every flourish, every nickname, and every bit. Just his name, or just hers, in lowercase.

- **Setup: The Ring Light / The Shadow.** Early on he boasts about his "studio lighting setup" for his mysterious app work.  
  **Middle:** The truth comes out—it’s a £14 clip light on the headboard so he doesn't have to see his own silhouette on the plasterboard when he’s on top.  
  **Payoff:** Tied directly to how they talk about fucking at the end—turning the big light on, or being seen without the angle.

## 7. How the Chat Ends

### If They Connect (The Earned Win)
The match sees through the top hat and the Noel Coward routine, refuses to laugh at his pre-emptive self-own, and corners him into saying one plain true sentence—the thing he couldn't do for Delphine.  
When Reuben drops the act, **his typing changes completely**. The capitals, the clauses, the stage-bows vanish. It costs him everything not to make a joke right after saying something naked, and we watch him visibly bite his own hand to stop typing a third message.

> Match: say one thing to me without the hat on
> Match: and if you make a joke I’m unmatching
> Reuben_404: I’ve been in my flat since the 14th
> Reuben_404: I’d really like to come out for a drink with you

### If They Crash (The Inevitable Loss)
The match actually likes him and touches the exact sore spot—offering him quietness, or asking to see him without the routine, or being a little too gentle after he reveals something humiliating (the pump, the fake app, the curve).  
The gentleness triggers his "we can just watch something" panic. Convinced he’s being pitied, he reaches for his biggest, brassiest, most obnoxious bit to prove he isn't hurt—landing squarely on whatever the match's own insecurity is. She closes her face, just like Delphine did, and he tips his hat to an empty room.

> Match: Reuben. stop. you don't have to do a bit right now
> Reuben_404: A bit? Darling, this is the director's cut. Wait till you see the outtakes, there's a whole sequence with a calf cramp and a Persian rug that won awards at Cannes.
> Reuben_404: Tough crowd tonight.
> Reuben_404: Take care of yourself out there 🎩

## 8. The Four-Question Check (What the Reader Must Know by the End)

1. **What is he afraid of?** That if he stops performing for eleven seconds, you’ll see how small and crooked and lonely he actually is, and you’ll be kind to him out of pity before you leave.
2. **What does he do so he doesn't have to feel it?** He narrates his own life like a charming raconteur, wears a stupid top hat, and makes the joke about himself five seconds before you can.
3. **Where does that come from?** Growing up cropped out of the frame by a family who can't remember which brother he is, a body that caught wrong in the dark followed by *"we can just watch something,"* and an ex (Delphine) who asked for one un-performed sentence and got two jokes instead.
4. **What did he do at the end?** Either he finally managed to type the one plain sentence he owed Delphine and left it on the screen without a punchline—or he heard kindness in the match's voice, panicked, and tap-danced himself straight out of the match.
