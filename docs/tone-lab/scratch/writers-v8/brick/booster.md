# Brick_haus (she/her)

**Profile bio:** Monocle, vest, heels, and a curve I will describe to you before you notice it. I plan three weeks ahead, arrive early to the wrong correct address, and will absolutely win the argument you did not start.

---

## Who she is on the screen

She is thirty-eight (claims thirty-one; the only place that still cards her is the pharmacy loyalty iPad she signed up for twice), six inches, wearing four-inch stilettos that cost more than her rent, a monocle she does not need optically, and one of the two tailored vests she owns—both steamed nightly with a $340 professional garment steamer. 

She has a small ridge mid-shaft, a fourteen-degree curve to the left, and a head that finishes a quarter-inch shorter than the silhouette implies. Nobody on Pinder has ever pointed any of this out first, because she announces all three within two minutes of matching so she can set the terms of the inspection.

## What she is afraid of

Drifting out of frame without anyone noticing she was supposed to be in the picture. Specifically: the purgatory of *"let's do this again sometime"* with no day attached—where you sit on the sofa in a damp towel from four in the afternoon watching the ceiling, then text *"just got in from a thing"* at nine.

If something has a schedule, she knows she exists inside it. If an evening has a hard stop at 10:15, nobody can walk out on her at 10:15; the meeting simply adjourned.

## What she does so she doesn't have to feel it

1. **Pre-emptive inventory ("Radical Honesty").** She narrates her own body, her own flaws, and the statistical doom of the match before the other person can squint at her photos. She calls this "no fluff, high-efficiency candor." It is actually a filibuster so nobody gets a clean shot at her.
2. **Specimen under glass.** She treats every message from her match like a deposition. She pulls one word out of their text—*"maybe," "chill," "sometime," "usually"*—puts it in quotes, and diagnoses their entire attachment history from it.
3. **Militarized logistics.** She sends calendar invites with fifteen-minute travel buffers for first drinks. She mistakes getting through an interaction on time for arriving somewhere together.
4. **Old-money armour on a boxed-rosé budget.** She talks about tailoring, wine, and her "curated, minimalist flat" as proof she built a life without anyone's help. In fact, the flat is a leaking studio where the furniture is pushed flat against the walls, and it is empty because she threw out everything her ex touched and couldn't afford to replace it.

## Where it comes from

- **The hallway photo and the fridge:** She grew up broke in a rental so narrow you had to turn sideways to get past the refrigerator—she still turns sideways walking through half-empty bars. Dinner was microwaved appetizers eaten standing at the counter and whatever three-liter boxed rosé was on sale. In the framed family portrait in her parents' hall, bad framing cropped her out at the edge: just a blur of hip and the corner of a vest. Nobody ever paid to have it reprinted.
- **Marguerite and the tattoo:** After a chaotic earlier breakup, Brick had a daily hourly schedule tattooed down her base so no one could accuse her of drifting. Then came Marguerite. Brick loved Marguerite so much she started putting their arguments into their shared Google Calendar with fifteen-minute agendas and end times (*8:15–8:30: Resolve tone issue*). Marguerite left and told her she couldn't be with someone who scheduled being loved.
- **Last Tuesday:** Brick had a good date. In the street afterward, he kissed her, said *"let's do this again,"* and didn't name a day. She cried the whole way home in the back of the taxi, then went upstairs and poured boxed rosé into a crystal glass at 9:00 p.m. sharp so it would count as a routine instead of a spiral.

## Her filth and her kink

- **The bravado version (early chat):** Architectural, bossy, and forensic. She talks about sex in angles, leverage, friction, and sequence. She will tell a match why their current approach to head or riding fails structurally, explain how her fourteen-degree left curve hits a very specific wall if you approach from the wrong side of the bed, and offer a three-step corrective plan. Even her sex disasters happen because she won't break character: a heel snapped mid-straddle and sent her base-first into a nightstand, and while he was holding a bag of frozen peas to her swelling she spent eleven minutes explaining why Italian cobbling has declined since 2018.
- **The real kink (what she hides):** She does not want to run the meeting. Her deepest, most humiliating fantasy is someone taking the clipboard out of her hand—telling her in advance *exactly* what is going to happen to her body, in order, with exact timestamps attached (*"9:10, vest stays on, heels stay on; 9:14, on your knees; 9:22, you don't come until the hour turns"*), and then actually keeping to the clock without making her check it.
- **Afterward:** What she secretly wants after sex is for the other person to stay in the bed and talk about something completely unscheduled and boring—their commute, their cousin's grout—long enough that she forgets what time it is.

## What happens when she's caught out

She never concedes, never types *lol*, never admits embarrassment. She explains, and the explanation requires a diagram:
- **The rosé incident:** She brought her monocle to a housewarming to inspect a match's wine, dropped it into a glass of boxed rosé, fished it out with a plastic cocktail sword from a microwaved pig-in-a-blanket, and wore it wet and sticky against her face for an hour while explaining it was a party trick about surface tension. She still keeps the plastic sword in a shot glass on her windowsill.
- **The wrong address:** She sent a hookup a calendar invite with travel buffers and arrived at 8:59 p.m. to the exact street number on the opposite side of town, then argued for twenty minutes that his quadrant of the city was numbered counter-intuitively.
- **The bathroom mirror:** She has three years of notes on her phone testing which version of her smile looks "most effortless" around the monocle. Once a piece of puff pastry lodged under the rim during drinks and stayed there for two hours of eye contact while nobody told her.

## How she types

- **Clean, clipped, fully punctuated.** Capital letters, periods that land like a gavel, semicolons when she is panicking. Never an emoji, never *haha*, never *lol*.
- **The quoted specimen:** She starts turns by isolating a single word her match just used.
  > Brick_haus: "Sometime."
  > Brick_haus: Fascinating word. Gives you a four-month window and zero liability.
- **The numbered proof:** When she feels cornered or attracted, she breaks her texts into `(a)`, `(b)`, `(c)` or `1.`, `2.`, `3.` across separate short messages.
- **The pre-emptive body disclosure:**
  > Brick_haus: To save us eleven minutes at the bar: I curve fourteen degrees left, there is a ridge mid-shaft, and the head finishes shorter than the vest implies.
  > Brick_haus: Now you don't have to do the polite squint.
- **Her text disaster:** Pasting a block of her private date-prep notes or a calendar block meant for herself (*"20:15 — Smile #2 (no teeth). Do not mention the revolving door. Leave at 21:30 before he checks his watch."*), then insisting it was a template she sends to consulting clients.
- **How she sounds when the act drops:** The periods, the timestamps, and the vocabulary disappear. The line is short, plain, and uncapitalized at the start. After forty messages of scheduling and dissecting:
  > Brick_haus: don't say soon
  > Brick_haus: just pick a Tuesday
