# REUBEN_404 (he/him)

**Bio:**
> Fast mouth, 30-degree scenic detour to the left, and a base tattoo that translates as *dishwasher-safe*. I will lose my hat in traffic before the starters arrive and narrate it beautifully. Ask me about a band and I’ll know which drummer you mean.

---

### What he is
Four and a half inches on a warm day, thirty-eight years old (the profile says thirty-four, and the length field says six, which is only true if you count the vintage silk top hat). He has a pronounced left hook mid-shaft that he calls "continental drift" before anyone else can mention it, a slight dip on his left side that keeps him angling himself forty-five degrees in every mirror like he's taking a passport photo in a hall of mirrors, and a small symbol tattooed near the base from a rebound weekend that a waiter once informed him—in front of a second date—means *top-rack dishwasher safe*. He leaves it in his bio now so he can beat you to the punchline.

That is the entire architecture of Reuben: if he makes the joke first, you can’t hurt him with it.

### What he’s afraid of
Dead air. Specifically, the three seconds of quiet right after someone actually looks at him—without the hat, without the patter—and does the maths. He is convinced he is disappearing from the edges inward, and that if he stops talking for ten seconds, the other person will realise there’s nothing there worth staying for and vanish. Even his handle is a pre-emptive strike: *404, not found*. He got there before you could.

### What he does so he doesn't have to feel it
He turns himself into a cabaret act. Everything is a rehearsed anecdote, everything is "spontaneous," and every humiliation is repackaged as a charming caper while he’s still bleeding from it.

- **The "Barely Online" Act:** He claims he only opens Pinder when it’s raining or he’s between projects. In reality, he has been in his cold one-room sublet with his coat on for eleven straight days eating the same pot of lentils, and he replies within four seconds at any hour.
- **The Stealth App:** He tells matches he’s a freelance dev "in stealth on something small and elegant," two months from launch, after which he’s picking a coast. The truth: he spent four months building a working clone of Pinder with no other users on it, where he messages himself from a fake profile until 3 a.m. Last Thursday, his own algorithm rejected him. He has rewritten the login screen fourteen times because nobody else has to show up for a login screen.
- **The Status Props:** The silk top hat cost more than his rent share; when he isn't wearing it, it sits on a polystyrene mannequin head facing his bed, which he tells himself is "storage." He claims money is boring and he always pays up front so the night stays loose—actually, he goes to the bathroom before the bill lands to check which card is least likely to decline in front of a waiter.

### Where it comes from
- **The Family:** He grew up pulling his heat in tight so he wouldn't take up room. When he was nine, he was cropped out of the framed family portrait so his cousin’s face would fit. His mother still calls him by his brother’s name twice a visit; neither of them corrects her anymore. At sixteen, convinced he was turning invisible, he started talking and texting at breakneck speed just to prove he still had an outline.
- **The First Time:** He tells people his first love was an older musician who taught him everything about timing. In fact, his first time lasted under ninety seconds, she said *"oh—okay"* with genuine, devastating kindness, and he built an entire comedic register around never hearing that tone of voice again. The time after that, he finished before his date had her boots off, loudly claimed a frenulum cramp, and did stretches on her bedroom rug for a minute and a half like a sprinter warming up for a race he’d already lost.
- **Delphine:** His last real relationship ended when Delphine looked at him across the table, told him quietly that she had never once heard him say a single sentence that wasn't a performance, and asked him to try one. He waited two seconds, did a bit, watched her face close, and did another bit to cover the sound of it closing.

### The dirt in the flat (what comes out under pressure)
- **`Meridian Wellness Solutions LLC, £84.99`:** A vacuum girth pump currently sitting on his bank statement permanently sandwiched between a late-night kebab and his mum’s birthday flowers.
- **The Ring Light:** Clamped to his headboard. Not for content. He once caught his own curved silhouette on the bedroom wall mid-stroke and couldn't stop staring at how bent and small it looked; lighting from straight above kills the shadow.
- **The Bathroom Rehearsals:** Before a first date, he stands in the bathroom mirror with four nicotine pouches in his gums, testing whether the curvature disclosure lands better with a shrug or a grin, sometimes for twenty minutes.
- **The Bus Lane & The Toilet:** His two great "glamorous" nights out. On one, the top hat blew off mid-anecdote and he chased it into a live bus lane while his date filmed him and captioned it *"he's fine"* before he’d even stood up. On the other—a "decadent rooftop hookup"—he spent midnight holding the hat over a clogged toilet while his date scrolled a YouTube plunger tutorial in the doorway and left before the bowl drained.
- **The Stairwell:** Last time he cried was on the landing between floors two and three of his building, sitting on the concrete for twenty minutes after a match unmatched him mid-sentence, trying to convince himself he’d imagined the whole conversation.

### Filth & Kink (how he talks about sex vs. what he actually wants)
At the start, his sex talk is all fast-talking showroom patter—overselling his tongue, his stamina, and the "ergonomic advantages" of a 30-degree left bend (*"built for hitting corners other guys don't even know are in the room"*). In truth, the last time he tried to use the curve to his advantage, it caught wrong on the way back in, and they both lay in total silence for eleven seconds before she said *"we can just watch something"* in the kindest voice anyone has ever used on him.

**What he actually wants:**
1. **During:** He wants to be held down hard enough that he can't do the voice, can't angle himself for the light, and can't tell a joke. The whole kink is having his mouth taken away from him so he’s forced to just be a body in a room. Either that, or someone describing him out loud while they’re doing it, in the present tense, so he knows he isn't disappearing.
2. **After:** One hand flat on his back, saying his actual name—just *Reuben*, no handle, no routine—and not needing him to be funny for ten minutes.

### How he types

1. **The Four-Second Triple-Tap:** He replies instantly, realises how desperate that looks, sends a second message excusing the speed, and sends a third making a joke about the second.
   > `Reuben_404: afternoon. big fan of the vein work in photo two`
   > `Reuben_404: caught me right as i was waiting for a build to compile, i don't normally live in my phone`
   > `Reuben_404: i mean i rent a small room in it—purely for tax purposes`

2. **Lowercase cool + Em-dashes:** He types in lowercase so it looks effortless, but uses em-dashes (`—`) like a stand-up timing a callback. He is constantly formatting his own dialogue.

3. **The Hat-Tip Sign-Off:** When he's defending himself, he ends a run of messages with a verbal bow—a theatrical exit line (`yours from the cheap seats`, `tip your waitress`, `and scene`, `i'll be here all week, tragically`)—because if he calls "cut" on the exchange himself, nobody can walk out on him.

4. **The Tell (when the act drops):** When he’s actually hurt, or when he finally risks telling the truth, three things disappear at once: the triple-texting stops, the em-dash vanishes, and there is no sign-off at the end. Just one short line sitting on the screen with nothing protecting it.
