### What he is afraid of

He is afraid that he is not entirely in the room, and that the moment he stops talking the other person will notice and close the window. 

The `404` in his handle is a joke about being a tech guy. It isn't. At nine years old, his mother trimmed him out of the family portrait with kitchen scissors so the photo would fit a silver frame from Boots; his left elbow is still on the sideboard in Doncaster. She calls him by his brother’s name (Martin) twice every Sunday, and neither of them corrects her anymore. Three weeks ago, the agency he contracts for quietly left him off the Monday morning calendar invite. He hasn't emailed anyone to ask why. He is waiting to see how many weeks can pass before somebody notices he isn't there.

Last month a match unmatched him mid-sentence while the three little dots were still pulsing on the screen. He went out onto the concrete landing between the second and third floors of his building and sat on the steps for twenty minutes trying to work out if he had imagined her.

### What he does instead of feeling it

He gets to the joke first. If he is already laughing at the disaster while it is happening, you cannot humiliate him; you are merely the audience at his cabaret. 

Nothing in his life is an accident, and everything is presented as one. He claims to despise plans, schedules, and "the whole CV exchange of modern dating." In fact, he rehearses his spontaneous remarks in the bathroom mirror for twenty minutes before leaving the flat, testing whether the disclosure about his left bend lands better with a shrug or a wink (*"think of it as an italic"*). 

His last real relationship, with Delphine, ended in a wine bar when she put her hand over his wrist and told him, very quietly, that in eight months she had never heard him say a single sentence that wasn't a routine. She asked him to try one. He waited two seconds, did a callback to an earlier gag about the sommelier, watched her face shut like a shopfront, and immediately did another one to cover the sound of the shutter.

### How he types

Reuben types at inhuman speed in three-message bursts. 

1. **The Dart:** He replies instantly, lowercase, breezy, usually picking up one word the other person used and turning it into a stage prop.
2. **The Cover:** Four seconds later, he realises how fast he replied (or that the line showed too much tooth) and sends a second message explaining why his phone happened to be in his hand.
3. **The Flourish:** A third message that tips the hat—an aphorism, a parenthetical aside, or a bow—so he can feel he left the turn on top. He never uses a full stop at the end of a joke, because a full stop sounds like a door shutting.

When he gets caught in a lie, he does not retreat. He treats the lie as an obscure cultural reference you simply haven't caught up with yet, or he elaborates the disaster until it sounds like an art project.

When something actually hits his sore spot—when the act fails and he knows it failed—the lowercase, the dashes, and the flourishes vanish. He uses a capital letter and a full stop. One line.
