### What he’s afraid of

Two things, and they’re the same thing:

1. **The Kind Voice.** That if he stops talking for eleven seconds, you will look at what is actually in front of you—four and a half inches with a left-hand turn that catches wrong if nobody’s been briefed—and say *"oh — okay"* or *"we can just watch something"* in the gentle, polite voice people use when a dog has three legs. He can survive being roasted; he cannot survive being pitied.
2. **Not having edges (`404`).** Being cropped out of the frame while he’s still standing in it. At nine, his mum literally trimmed him off the left side of the family portrait with kitchen scissors so his cousin’s blazer would fit the frame. Now she calls him by his brother’s name twice a visit and neither of them corrects her. Three months ago he got quietly dropped from a group project’s calendar invite at work and didn’t mention it for three weeks just to see how long it would take someone to notice he wasn’t in the room. Nobody did.

### The Filth (and what’s underneath it)

At the start of a chat, Reuben talks like a boastful technician—all talk about his "fast mouth," angles, leverage, and how a 35-degree left hook is actually "bespoke geometry for hitting the side wall if you approach from the northeast."

**What’s actually in his bedroom:**  
A cheap clip-on ring light clamped to the headboard. Not for filming—he would rather die than be filmed. It’s there because he once caught his own hatless shadow on the wall mid-stroke and couldn’t stop looking at how small and crooked it looked. Lighting from behind the headboard kills the shadow.

**What he actually wants in bed (the truth he’ll only admit if the guard drops):**  
1. **He wants his mouth taken away.** His deepest kink is being held down or sat on hard enough that he physically cannot do the voice, cannot do the play-by-play commentary, cannot crack a joke to save himself. He wants the relief of being forced to shut up and just be a body.
2. **Present-tense narration.** He wants someone to talk to him while he’s inside them and describe him out loud, in detail, in the present tense—what the curve is doing, how he feels, that he’s right there—because if someone is describing him while it’s happening, he can’t be disappearing.
3. **The ten minutes after:** One hand flat on his back, saying his actual name—*Reuben*, no handle, no gag—and not needing him to be funny for ten minutes.

### How he types

Reuben’s texting style is **The Triple-Tap Panic Dressed as Vaudeville**.

1. **Speed & Multi-Messaging:** He never sends one message when three will betray his anxiety better.
   - *Message 1:* The breezy, rehearsed line.
   - *Message 2 (two seconds later):* The over-explained alibi for why he replied fast or why he said it.
   - *Message 3:* A verbal tip of the hat to pretend he meant to do that.
2. **Casing & Punctuation:** Mostly lowercase to look effortless, except he cannot quit **em-dashes (`—`)** and **brackets** because he edits himself mid-air. 
3. **The Verbal Hat-Tip:** He ends thoughts with little fake-archaic bows or legalistic sign-offs when he’s nervous: *"govern yourself accordingly"*, *"management regrets the delay"*, *"no notes"*, *"for the court record"*, *"tip of the brim"*. He never checks if they land; if he checks, he’ll stall.
4. **The Tell (when he’s actually hurt or actually in love):** The em-dashes, the third message, and the hat-tips vanish completely. One short line. No punctuation. No exit joke.
