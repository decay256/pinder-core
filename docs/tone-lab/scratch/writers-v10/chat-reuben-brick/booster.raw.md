## The bit

1. **The idea.** The premise here is competitive preemptive disclosure—what happens when two people whose entire survival strategy is "I have to confess what’s wrong with my body first so you can’t make a face when my pants come off" match with each other and fight over the microphone. When I’m looking for a bit, especially about dating apps, I always start with the lie we tell ourselves that we’re just being "self-aware" or "upfront," when really we’re trying to control the room so nobody can hurt our feelings. What caught me in the material for Reuben and Brick_haus is that they have the exact same defense mechanism—getting ahead of their weird, stubby, curved silhouettes so they never get pitied or cropped out—but they’re performing it in two completely incompatible genres. Reuben is doing a sweaty, triple-texting vaudeville tight five in a top hat, and Brick_haus is running a hostile McKinsey audit in a monocle and heels. So first I’m exaggerating that collision: Reuben throws out his rehearsed bathroom-mirror jokes expecting a pity-preventing laugh, and Brick_haus refuses to laugh and instead grades his insecurities like a spreadsheet. Then the turn comes from looking at the specific bedroom humiliations they’re hiding and realizing they fit together like a lock and key: Reuben’s deepest sexual shame is finishing in under two minutes before a girl even gets her boots off, and Brick_haus is a woman with an hourly schedule tattooed on her shaft who gets off on rigid, timestamped fifteen-minute calendar blocks. That sets up a final turn where, even after they drop the act and book a real date in pen, she still has to audit the leftover minutes in the calendar block.

2. **Why it's funny.** There are two big gaps where the laughs live. First, in the setup, when someone opens on an app with self-deprecating humor about their dick, the social contract says you either play along with a "haha" or politely reassure them. Brick_haus does neither—she treats his stand-up bit like a defective product disclosure and counters with her own ruthless anatomical spec sheet. Watching a guy try to tap-dance for someone who replies like a corporate lawyer is funny because his rhythm keeps hitting a brick wall. The second, bigger laugh is the status reversal at the end: the reader expects a guy confessing to premature ejaculation to either get rejected or get patted on the head, which is what Reuben braces for. Instead, Brick_haus reacts to a guaranteed ninety-second runtime like she just found a unicorn who respects her calendar, turning a humiliating confession into a dominant dirty-talk schedule—until her compulsion to account for all fifteen minutes of the calendar block undercuts the romance on the last beat.

3. **Why it's Pinder.** Dating apps have turned "radical transparency" and "knowing what you want" into a neurotic arms race. People put their trauma, their red flags, and their body issues in the first three messages with a straight face and call it "saving time," when really they’re terrified of the five seconds of silence where a stranger decides if they’re good enough. And on the flip side, everyone uses fake-breezy words like *"sometime"* to keep one foot out the door while pretending they’re just busy. Making Reuben and Brick_haus literal four-inch dicks in vests and hats who are negotiating shaft curvature, dishwasher tattoos, and fifteen-minute sex blocks makes that whole app ritual look as unhinged as it actually is: two lonely people trying to sign a liability waiver before they let themselves be touched.
