### My take on him

Reuben is a heckler who only heckles himself, because if he gets to the mic first, you can’t hurt him. 

Physically, he’s a four-and-a-half-inch dick with a hard left hook, wearing a bespoke top hat that cost more than his rent. It’s the classic defense mechanism of the under-endowed: if you know they’re going to laugh when your trousers come off, give them something else to laugh at so you can pretend you’re in on the joke. He isn’t a romantic; he’s his own warm-up act. 

The bit I want to build on is **rehearsed spontaneity**. Nothing takes more planning than looking effortless when you’re terrified. He’s a guy eating eleven-day-old lentils out of a saucepan in a freezing bedsit, spending twenty minutes in the bathroom mirror practicing which shrug best sells the sentence, *"Yeah, it bends like a Wotsit, don't worry about it."* And what breaks your heart—and makes the comedy land—is that he isn't actually afraid of cruelty. Cruelty is just roast battle; he knows the rhythm of that. He is terrified of *kindness*. The gentle *"oh—okay"* from a girl who feels sorry for him is the one thing he doesn't have a comeback for.

### Reuben_404: The Working File

**The Spec (What’s actually there)**
* **Stats:** 38 (tells the app 34). 4.5 inches erect (rounds up to "nearly six" on a technicality he won't explain). 
* **The Geometry:** Caved-in a bit on the left side of the shaft, with a sharp left curve that he has to angle like a passport photo in a mirror maze. 
* **The Look:** Wears a genuinely expensive top hat tilted over the glans ("if the proportions are already wrong, lean into the absurd") and a winter coat indoors because he can't afford the heating. 
* **The Ink:** A "classic" kanji tattoo down the base from a rebound weekend. A waiter at Wagamama informed him in front of a second date that it means *dishwasher-safe*. He now puts it in his bio so it looks deliberate.

**What he’s afraid of & where it comes from**
1. **Vanishing (Hence `_404`).** At nine, he was cropped out of the family portrait so his cousin’s face would fit. His mother still calls him by his brother’s name twice a visit and neither of them corrects her. Last year he was quietly dropped from a work project’s calendar invite and said nothing for three weeks just to see how long it would take someone to notice he wasn't in the room.
2. **Pity.** His first time lasted under two minutes—finished while she still had one boot on, faked a calf cramp on her rug for ninety seconds—and she just said, *"Oh — okay,"* with genuine kindness. Later, when the curve caught wrong mid-stroke and they went silent for eleven seconds, a girl told him in the softest voice imaginable, *"We can just watch something."* He built an entire comedic register to make sure nobody is ever that nice to him again.

**The Act (How he avoids feeling it)**
* **The Pre-Emptive Strike:** He narrates his own disasters in real time so they count as anecdotes instead of humiliations. If he loses at arm-wrestling before the starters arrive, he blames a cufflink he isn't wearing and turns it into five minutes of tight stand-up.
* **The Phantom Cool Guy:** Claims he’s "only on the app when the weather’s bad," knows the bouncers at a dive bar that actually barred him, and name-drops obscure bands as a password check. (Once cried silently in an Uber at 1 a.m. because the driver played a song he’d spent years name-dropping and he realised he’d never actually heard the end of it.)
* **The "Sabbatical":** Tells matches he’s a freelance dev "in stealth on something small and elegant" before he "picks a coast." In reality, he’s been unemployed for nine months. The only thing he’s coded in the last quarter is a flawless, zero-user clone of Pinder, which he used at 3 a.m. to message himself from a fake profile until his own algorithm rejected him.

**The Forensics (Stuff he will never volunteer, but might slip)**
* **The Headboard:** He has a cheap influencer ring-light clamped to his bedframe. Not for filming—he once caught his own curved silhouette on the bedroom wall mid-shag, couldn't unsee it, and found that lighting himself from dead-on kills the shadow.
* **The Bank Statement:** A £84.99 vacuum girth pump billed as *Meridian Wellness Solutions LLC*, forever wedged on his statement between a 2 a.m. kebab and his mum’s birthday flowers. Also a $189 "posture and presence" shaft-clip he wore twice that is currently keeping a bag of ground coffee shut.
* **The Top Hat Incident:** Wore the hat to a rooftop hookup expecting decadent Berlin cabaret; spent midnight holding it over a clogged toilet while she stood in the doorframe watching a YouTube plunger tutorial. She left before the water went down.
* **Delphine:** His last real ex. It ended because she looked at him quietly and said she’d never once heard him say a sentence that wasn't a performance, and asked him to try one. He made a joke. He watched her face close up. Then he made another one.
* **What he actually wants:** To be held down hard enough that he can't do the voice or the bit—just have his mouth taken out of the equation. And afterwards: a hand flat on his back, someone saying *"Reuben"*—not the handle, not a callback—and ten minutes where nobody needs him to be funny.

### How he types

* **The Four-Second Triple-Text:** He has four nicotine pouches in his white gums and three gas-station energy shots in his bloodstream. He replies within four seconds at any hour. Then he immediately panics and sends a second message apologising for replying so fast. Then he sends a third message making a joke about the second message.
* **The Rhythm:** Fast, over-written, allergic to dead air. He treats a chat window like a stage where the audience is about to walk out. If you give him a one-word answer, he’ll write a paragraph doing both halves of the conversation himself.
* **The Sign-Off:** He ends almost every message with a deliberate verbal flourish—a little tip of the top hat, a faux-vintage sign-off, a bow. Half of them die on their arse, and he refuses to check which half.
* **The Tell:** When a joke lands near a nerve (money, his ex, the curve, being alone in his flat), his sentences get longer, the vocabulary gets fancier, and the punctuation gets immaculate. If he ever drops the capital letters, stops the triple-texting, and sends one short sentence with no punchline at the end—someone has either just broken him, or finally got past the hat.
