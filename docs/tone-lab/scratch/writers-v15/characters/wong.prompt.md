## The rule about the filth

We can be as gross and cynical as we want about the app, and we can roast the hell out of the armor people put on to survive it. We are **never** cynical about the fact that they want to be loved. 

A filthy joke only works if it gives away something true that the person didn’t mean to show you. Underneath the dick pic and the bragging and the weird little fedora, these have to feel like two real people who want something real and are doing a terrible job of asking for it. The comedy comes from the gap between the big talk and the terrified little freak underneath.

## What happens to the person reading it

Somebody opens their phone and reads one of our chats. That’s all they get. In five minutes, here’s what has to happen to them:

First, they laugh at the hustle. Both of these dicks come in doing a bit—some weird, hyper-specific habit or flex they use to control the room. It’s ridiculous, and we laugh at them for it.

Then, we let slip *why* they do it. We see the bruise underneath the flex. You don’t stop laughing, but now the laugh catches in your throat a little, because oh shit, you’ve done a version of that too. 

Then we watch their two acts crash straight into each other.

**If they end up together:** It has to feel earned. We have to watch both of them get scared, and then choose to put their weapon down anyway. It’s still funny, it’s still dirty, but it’s tender—and the reader has to know why it could only be *these* two weirdos and nobody else.

**If they crash and burn:** The reader should laugh, wince, and think, *Yep, that’s why everyone is single.* Nobody is the bad guy. In fact, the reader should be able to see that these two actually would’ve been great together. What kills it is that the exact shield Person A uses to protect themselves lands right on Person B’s open wound. The act, the fear, and the app win. 

We don’t care if a chat ends in a date or an unmatch. A date that feels fake is a failure. A brutal unmatch that makes the reader laugh and think about their own ex for three days is a massive win.

## How you know if your chat works

1. **What are they terrified of?**
2. **What is the bullshit act they put on so they don’t have to feel it?**
3. **Where did that come from?**
4. **What did they do at the very end?**

If the reader can’t answer all four for both people, I don’t care how hard the jokes killed—the chat didn't work, and we’re rewriting it.
