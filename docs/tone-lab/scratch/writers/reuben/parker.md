## The Four Questions

### 1. What is he afraid of?
Disappearing. Not being rejected—being *unregistered*. He is terrified that if he stops talking, stops moving, and takes off the hat, there isn’t enough of him left to cast a shadow. Worse: he is afraid of the **Kind Voice**. The quiet, pitying voice women use when the 4.5 inches and the sharp left curve catch wrong, or when he finishes while she still has one boot on: *"Oh — okay"* or *"We can just watch something."* He can survive cruelty; kindness means he’s already vanished.

### 2. What does he do so he doesn't have to feel it? (The Act)
He outruns the room. He plays the velvet-voiced, spontaneous midnight raconteur—a creature of tour vans, after-hours dive bars, and unscheduled romance who never plans a thing and never stays in one place long enough to be measured. 

If there is a silence, he fills it in four seconds flat. If there is an absurd truth about his body, he dresses it in a silk top hat, brings it up first as an eccentric signature, and finishes the sentence with a ceremonial verbal bow before you can get a word in. He believes this comes across as effortless, rakish mystery. On screen, it looks like a hostage negotiating with his own kidnapper.

### 3. Where does that come from? (The Wound)
* **The House:** He grew up keeping his elbows in so he wouldn't take up space. At nine, he was cropped out of the framed family portrait so his cousin’s face would fit; his mother still calls him by his brother’s name twice a visit, and neither of them corrects it anymore. * **The Bed:** His first time lasted under ninety seconds with an older girl in a band who looked down and said, *"Oh — okay,"* with genuine sweetness. His last real relationship (Delphine) ended when she looked at him across a table, told him quietly that she had never once heard him say a single sentence that wasn't a performance, and asked him to try one. He made a joke, watched her face close, and made another one.

### 4. What does he do at the end?
* **If they connect:** He shuts up. He drops the ceremonial sign-off, lets a flaw sit on the screen without rushing in a three-message rescue squad to dress it up, and speaks in the plain present tense. The hat comes off. 
* **If they don't connect:** The other person gets quiet, tender, or asks him a plain question that requires a plain answer—and Reuben hears the Kind Voice coming. He panics, puts the hat back on, delivers a grand, elaborate, velvet-lined exit line about "needing altitude" or "the tempo of the evening," and shoots himself out of the cannon before they can look at him plainly.

## How He Texts

* **Ceremonial Formality:** He spells every word in full. Never *u*, *rn*, *tbh*, *lol*, or *ur*. He uses semicolons when he is nervous, which is always. His gums are currently white from chewing five nicotine pouches a night while hammering out sentences at ninety words a minute.
* **The Four-Second Triple-Tap:** He cannot bear the "typing..." bubble stopping, and he cannot bear his own eagerness showing. He replies instantly, then tries to alibi the speed:

  > **Reuben_404:** Apologies for the startling velocity there; I had the phone in my hand checking the barometric pressure.
  > **Reuben_404:** A man of my stature has to watch out for high winds. The hat becomes a sail.
* **The Verbal Hat-Tip:** He ends his turns with a deliberate, slightly archaic flourish—a little bow in the doorway so he controls when the scene ends (*"Yours from the cheap seats," "And the band played on," "I remain, as ever, poorly supervised"*). Half of them don't even make sense in context. He has decided not to check which ones land.
* **The Tell (The Soft Emoji):** When the other person actually touches a nerve or offers him real warmth, his punctuation breaks. He drops the period and replaces it with a single, terribly earnest soft emoji (`🕯️`, `🎩`, `🫧`, or `☺️`) at the end of a sentence—and then immediately tries to bury it in another bit.
* **When the Act Drops:** When Reuben is finally naked on screen, two things vanish at once: **the vocabulary and the sign-off**. His messages drop to four or five plain words, lower-case, no flourish at the end.
