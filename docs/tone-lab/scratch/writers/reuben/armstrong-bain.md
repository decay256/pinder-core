## 4. How He Texts

*   **The Rhythm:** Ceremonial, fully spelled-out formality used as armour, delivered at the speed of a man with five white nicotine pouches in his gums and four petrol-station energy shots in his bloodstream. He doesn't use "u" or "rn". He writes in complete, slightly theatrical clauses, and then sends three of them in seven seconds.
*   **The Verbal Hat-Tip:** He ends his turns with a deliberate, old-school flourish—a verbal bow or sign-off (*"Yours in questionable geometry," "The defense rests," "Govern yourself accordingly"*). Half of them don't quite make sense. He never checks which ones land; he just keeps moving.
*   **The Tell (When he’s rattled):** The triple-text spiral.
    1. An instant, over-eager reply.
    2. A breezy, unasked-for logistical lie explaining why he replied in two seconds.
    3. A self-deprecating flourish to cover the tracks.
*   **The Tell (When real warmth breaks through):** The ceremonial punctuation drops completely. When he forgets to do the voice—or when someone actually sees him and doesn't flinch—he replaces his full stops and verbal bows with a single, slightly dated soft emoji at the end of a plain sentence (`🙂` or `😌`). He only does this when the shield is down.
*   **His Text Disaster:** Pasting or sending a message meant for his cloned test-app—or accidentally pasting a line from his Notes app where he has pre-written three variations of a "spontaneous" reply (e.g., pasting all three options at once: *Option A: breezy / Option B: callback to the band / Option C: ask about the curve*, and then trying to claim it’s a "choose your own adventure" bit).

He burns the bridge just so he can be the one narrating the fire.
*   **How He Wins (The Earned Tenderness):** The other character refuses to laugh at his cover story and essentially holds him down in the text—telling him to shut up, take the hat off, and say one true thing.
