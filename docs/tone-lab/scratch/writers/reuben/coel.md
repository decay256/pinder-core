## The Core Engine

### 1. What he is afraid of
Disappearing while someone is looking right at him. Being seen plainly—4.5 inches, bent left, thirty-eight, skint—and met not even with cruelty, which he could riff on, but with gentle, polite pity.

### 2. Where it comes from
He grew up keeping his elbows in. At nine, he was cropped out of the framed family portrait so his cousin’s face would fit; his mother still calls him by his brother’s name twice a visit and neither of them corrects it anymore. Last month at work, he was quietly dropped from a project’s calendar invite and said nothing for three weeks just to see how long it would take someone to notice he wasn’t in the room. 

Sexually, it crystallized in two moments: his first time, with an older woman in a band who lasted ninety seconds with him before saying *"oh—okay"* with genuine kindness; and his most recent mishap, when his left curve caught wrong on the way back in, followed by eleven seconds of dead silence before she said, in the gentlest voice he has ever heard, *"we can just watch something."* He built an entire comedic personality to make sure nobody ever uses that voice on him again.

### 3. What he does so he doesn't have to feel it (The Act)
He beats everyone to the punchline. If he makes the proportions, the curve, and the tattoo into a cabaret act before you even take your coat off, you can’t reject him—you’re just watching his show. He plays the nocturnal, spontaneous, slightly dangerous man-about-town who treats dating apps like a joke he’s only half-paying attention to. He buried himself under so much flourish, ceremony, and high-speed patter that his last real ex, Delphine, finally ended it by telling him quietly that she had never once heard him say a single sentence that wasn't performing. She asked him to try one. He made a joke, watched her face close, and made another one.

### 4. What he actually needs
For someone to take his mouth away. His secret kink is being held down hard enough that he can’t do the voice, can’t do the bit, can’t tap-dance—just has to be a body in a bed. And after: one hand flat on his back, someone saying his actual name (*Reuben*, not the handle, not a nickname), and not needing him to be funny for ten straight minutes.

## The Gap: The Claims vs. The Flat

Reuben uses the language of effortless, curated cool ("spontaneity," "tempo," "keeping things unscheduled," "only on here when it rains") to hide a life of intense, lonely rehearsal.

*   **The App:** Claims he’s a freelance developer "two months from launching something small and elegant, then picking a coast." **In fact:** The only thing he has finished in four months is a working clone of Pinder with zero users, which he used exclusively to message himself from a fake profile until 3 a.m. on a Thursday when his own algorithm unmatched him.
*   **The Social Life:** Claims he "just got back from a trip," knows the door guys by name, and lives in a lively flat where friends are always crashing. **In fact:** He lives in a freezing one-room sublet where he wears his coat indoors, has been eating the same pot of lentils for eleven days, and got fired from eleven months of working the door at a dive bar after arguing with a regular he misidentified as a famous singer. (The actual singer later walked up to him at that same bar—in front of a date—and asked him politely to stop confusing him with his brother.)
*   **The Money:** Claims money is boring and he likes to pay upfront "so the night keeps its tempo." **In fact:** He times his bathroom trips at bars to check which debit card is least likely to decline, and his last bank statement has £84.99 to *Meridian Wellness Solutions LLC* (a vacuum girth pump currently wedged between a kebab charge and his mum’s birthday flowers) and $189 for a plastic "posture and presence" collarbone clip that is currently holding a bag of coffee shut.
*   **The Spontaneity:** Claims the best nights are entirely unplanned. **In fact:** He spends twenty minutes in front of the bathroom mirror before every first date rehearsing his "curvature disclosure" out loud—testing whether it lands better with a shrug or a grin—before running his three best lines in three accents and arm-wrestling his own left hand until the right one wins.

## How He Texts

*   **The Voice:** Ceremonial, fully spelled out, slightly ornate. He writes like a guy tipping a velvet hat at the end of every sentence—little verbal flourishes he throws out and refuses to check whether they landed.
*   **The Speed Panic:** He has five nicotine pouches in his white gums and four gas-station energy shots in his bloodstream. He replies within **four seconds** at any hour. Then he realizes how desperate that looks, sends a second message excusing the speed, and a third message making a joke about the second.
    > `Reuben_404: Evening. I would offer to take your coat, but we are both essentially necks in a phone.`
    > `Reuben_404: Apologies for the four-second draw, I had the screen open for a work thing.`
    > `Reuben_404: The work thing was staring at the wall. How are we.`
*   **The Password Check:** He will drop an obscure band era or a hyper-specific cultural crumb early on. He acts like a snob doing a vibe check, but it’s actually a password test—that band is tied to the last year he felt like a real person, and if you know it, he’s instantly defenceless.
*   **The Tell for Warmth:** When he is genuinely charmed and forgets to do the ceremony, his terminal punctuation turns into a single, slightly dated soft emoji (`☺️` or `😌`).
*   **The Text Disaster Waiting to Happen:** A paste-fail or wrong-window message from his 3 a.m. search history or his fake clone app. (e.g., accidentally pasting `"does left curvature actually affect depth or just angle"` or a test message he wrote to himself on his empty app to see how it looked on a screen).
*   **How Tenderness Sounds (Dropping the Act):** When Reuben finally puts the hat down, the vocabulary shrinks. The flourishes vanish. No clauses, no metaphors, no second and third cover-up messages. After forty messages of baroque, breathless tap-dancing, a naked Reuben line looks like:
    > `Reuben_404: Please don't go yet.`
    or
    > `Reuben_404: It's just Reuben.`

## How the Match Goes

*   **How he connects (The Win):** The other dick refuses to laugh at his self-deprecating setup, or interrupts his routine to look directly at the thing he’s hiding—the 4.5 inches, the left hook, the freezing flat, the fact that he’s been online all night—and stays anyway. If the match tells him to shut up and takes the wheel, Reuben folds like a deckchair. He stops pitching, lets the silence sit without filling it, and tells one true thing without a punchline.
*   **How he blows it (The Cynical Laugh):** The match touches his sore spot—shows him a flash of polite kindness, takes slightly too long to reply (triggering his "I'm being cropped out of the photo" panic), or asks a plain, earnest question like Delphine did. Reuben can't bear the exposure, so he reaches for the top hat, fires off a loud, defensive bit right into the middle of a tender moment, and watches them close up—proving to himself once again that he was right to keep his coat on.
