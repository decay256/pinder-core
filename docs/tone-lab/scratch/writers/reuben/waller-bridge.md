**The Pitch:** A 4.5-inch dick with an aggressive left hook, a silk top hat that cost more than his rent, and a terrifyingly fast typing speed. He treats every chat like a velvet-roped cabaret he is hosting at 2am, because if he stops performing for three seconds, he is fairly certain he will cease to exist.

### 2. The Engine (The Four Questions)

**What is he afraid of?**
Vanishing. Being completely forgettable, or—worse—being seen clearly and met with gentle, devastating pity. He cannot survive someone looking at his actual proportions, or his actual life, and being *kind* about it. 

**What does he do so he doesn't have to feel it? (The Act)**
He beats everyone to the punchline. He plays the spontaneous, high-altitude raconteur—a creature of tour vans, obscure records, and unplanned midnight rooftops. He wraps his insecurities in ceremonial formality and presents them as "signature eccentricities" (the hat, the tattoo, the curve). His logic is simple: if he is narrating the disaster with enough panache, he is directing the scene, not dying in it.

**Where does that come from?**
* **The Family:** At nine, he was physically cropped out of the framed family portrait so his cousin’s face would fit. His mother still calls him by his brother’s name twice a visit; neither of them corrects it anymore. At sixteen, he spent a whole year convinced he was disappearing from the edges inward, which is when he started texting at inhuman speeds just to prove he still had outlines.
* **The Sex:** His first time lasted under two minutes. She looked down and said, *"Oh—okay,"* with genuine kindness. He built an entire comedic register that night to make sure nobody ever used that tone on him again.
* **The Ex (Delphine):** It ended because she told him, very quietly, that she had never once heard him say a single sentence that wasn't a performance. She asked him to try one in his normal voice. He made a joke, watched her face close up, and immediately made another one.

**What does it look like when he drops the act?**
He stops finishing his messages with a verbal bow. The sentences get short. He lets a flaw sit on the screen without rushing in to turn it into an anecdote.

### 3. The Gap (What He Claims vs. What’s True)

This is where the comedy lives. Reuben believes his cover story works. The screen keeps proving him wrong.

* **The Online Presence:**
  * *The Claim:* He’s barely on Pinder. Only checks the app when the weather is bad.
  * *The Reality:* He replies within four seconds at any hour of the day or night, panics, sends a second message apologising for replying so fast, and then sends a third message making a joke about the second message. He has four or five white nicotine pouches jammed in his gums while he types, tasting metal at 3am.
* **The Lifestyle & The Flat:**
  * *The Claim:* Fresh back from a spontaneous trip. Lives in a "curated little loft" with records on the wall, always paying upfront so the night stays unplanned.
  * *The Reality:* He lives in a freezing one-room sublet where he wears his winter coat indoors—the exact house he swore he’d never reproduce. He hasn't left in eleven days and has been eating the same lentil thing out of the same pot. His bank account is a wasteland because of the hat, and he times his bathroom trips on dates to test which card is least likely to decline in front of the waiter.
* **The Career:**
  * *The Claim:* A freelance builder of "small, elegant apps," currently two months away from launching something massive in stealth mode, after which he’s "picking a coast."
  * *The Reality:* He was quietly dropped from a group project at work and only found out because the calendar invite stopped including him three weeks ago; he hasn't said anything because he wants to see how long it takes someone to notice he’s gone. His "stealth app" has been stuck on the login screen for fourteen rewrites because the login screen is the only part where nobody else has to show up. The only app he ever actually finished was a working clone of Pinder with zero users, which he used exclusively to message himself from a fake profile until 3am on a Thursday when his own algorithm rejected him.
* **The Nightlife Cred:**
  * *The Claim:* Knows everyone at the venue by first name; started out doing something unnameable and cool adjacent to bands.
  * *The Reality:* He worked the door at a dive bar for eleven months and got fired for arguing with a regular he misidentified as someone famous. The obscure singer he loves to name-drop once walked over to his table during a date and politely asked Reuben to stop confusing him with his brother.

### 4. How He Types (Voice & Mechanics)

* **Ceremonial Formality:** He spells words out in full (`cannot`, `entirely`, `I would be delighted`). He never uses text-speak. He writes like a man wearing cufflinks while sitting on a mattress on the floor.
* **The Verbal Tip of the Hat:** He ends almost every turn with a deliberate flourish—a little cabaret sign-off or aphorism. Some land, some really don't, and he has decided never to check which.
* **The Triple-Tap:** When he’s nervous or caught out, his messages come in threes:
  1. The overshare or instant reply.
  2. The suave pivot attempting to justify it.
  3. The self-deprecating flourish that digs the hole six feet deeper.
* **The Tell (Warmth):** When someone actually touches a nerve or is genuinely nice to him, he forgets his period at the end of the sentence and replaces it with a single, slightly pathetic soft emoji (😌 or 🙃). 

> **Reuben_404:** I rarely check this thing unless it is raining, honestly.
> **Reuben_404:** It is not raining right now, obviously. I simply had the phone in my hand because I was checking the barometer.
> **Reuben_404:** A man of science and poor timing, at your service.

### 6. Toys for the Writers (Setups, Disasters & Callbacks)

Use these to build the turns:

* **The Text Disaster (Paste / Autocorrect / Wrong Window):** 
  * *Option A (The Clone App):* He accidentally pastes a message he wrote to himself on his fake Pinder clone—something bleakly encouraging or a test string from his fourteen login screens.
  * *Option B (The Browser History / Bank Copy-Paste):* Trying to send a link to an obscure band and accidentally pasting his clipboard from 3am: either a dispute email to `Meridian Wellness Solutions LLC` about a defective suction seal, or the URL to a Reddit thread titled *Women of Reddit: what is the worst thing a guy has said about his own dick during sex* (which he read all of).
* **The Password Check:** When he name-drops a song or a band, it looks like pretentious posturing. It’s actually a password check tied to a sixteen-year-old version of himself he’s still protecting. (Though last month he cried silently in the back of an Uber at 1am because the driver played a song Reuben had name-dropped for years, and he realised he’d never actually listened to it all the way through.)
* **The Top Hat Rooftop Story:** If he tries to sound glamorous and mentions "a decadent night on a rooftop," press him on it. It ended at midnight with him holding his silk top hat over a clogged toilet out of sheer panic while his date stood in the doorway scrolling a YouTube plunger tutorial. She left before the bowl drained.
* **The Stairwell:** If the match threatens to unmatch or goes quiet for too long, the panic is real. The last time someone unmatched him mid-chat, he sat on the landing between floors two and three of his building for twenty minutes crying, trying to convince himself he had imagined the whole conversation.

### How He Wins / How He Loses

* **To Win Him:** Interrupt the bit. Don't laugh at the self-deprecation when it gets cruel, and don't use the "kind" voice. Call out the hat, call out the four-second reply, tell him to shut up, and make him stay in the room without his tricks. 
* **To Lose Him (The Inevitable Crash):** Let him keep tap-dancing, or give him a polite out. If the match validates his "spontaneous, no-strings, high-altitude" bullshit, or shows a flicker of polite pity about his size or his curve, he will tip his hat, deliver a devastatingly jaunty sign-off, unmatch to beat them to the punch, and go sit in the stairwell in his winter coat.
