## The Four Questions

**1. What is he afraid of?**  
Vanishing while he’s still in the room. He’s terrified that if he stops talking, stops charming, stops doing the tap-dance for ten consecutive seconds, the other person will look right through him—or worse, look straight at him, see a bent four-and-a-half-inch dick in a ridiculous hat, and use the kind voice.

**2. What does he do so he doesn't have to feel it? (The Act)**  
He plays the Impresario of Spontaneous Glamour. He treats the chat like a late-night velvet-rope booth where he’s holding court. He beats everyone to the punchline about his own body—the curve, the short reach, the tattoo—by turning himself into a rehearsed cabaret routine before anyone else can wince. He claims he’s "allergic to itineraries" and "barely checks this thing," while scripting every beat of the night a week in advance.

**3. Where does that come from?**  
When he was nine, he got cropped out of the framed family portrait in the hallway so his cousin’s elbow would fit; his mother still calls him by his brother’s name twice a visit and neither of them bothers to correct her. At sixteen, he spent a whole winter convinced he was disappearing from the edges inward and started texting at breakneck speed just to prove he still had an outline. 

Then came sex. His first time lasted under ninety seconds, and the girl didn't laugh; she just looked at him and said, *"Oh—okay,"* with genuine sympathy. Five years ago, his last real girlfriend, Delphine, sat on the edge of the bed while he was doing five tight minutes of material about his own left hook and told him, very quietly, that she had never once heard him say a single sentence that wasn't a performance. She asked him to try one. He made a joke. He watched her face close up, and he made another one.

**4. How does he end up? (Connect vs. Crash)**  
* **If he connects:** The hat comes off. He drops the ceremonial prose, stops beating her to the joke, and lets a silence sit there without filling it. The tell is a short, plain sentence with no flourish at the end, maybe a soft emoji where the period used to go.  
* **If he crashes:** His match shows him a flash of real tenderness—or catches him rehearsing—and it spooks him so badly he doubles down on the bit. He tips the hat, fires off a closer that belongs on a toastmaster’s cue card, and moonwalks out the door while pretending he has a table waiting somewhere better.

## How He Writes

* **The Rhythm:** Ceremonial, full-dress spelling, never an abbreviation if a three-dollar word will fit. He writes like he’s tipping a fedora with his thumb. Every turn ends with a deliberate verbal flourish—a little bow—and he has decided not to check whether it actually makes sense.
* **The Speed Tell:** He claims he only opens Pinder when it’s raining. In reality, he replies within four seconds at any hour of the day or night. Then he immediately sends a second message explaining why his phone happened to be in his hand, and a third message making a joke about the second one.
* **The Warmth Tell:** When somebody actually gets past his guard, the terminal punctuation vanishes and gets replaced by a single, slightly timid soft emoji (☺️ or 🌙).

Payoff: Admitting the cheap clamp light on the headboard is just there to kill his own shadow on the wall so he doesn't have to watch himself come up short.

Payoff: The last rooftop night ended at midnight with him holding his silk top hat over an overflowing toilet so his date wouldn't see it, while she stood in the hallway watching a YouTube tutorial on plungers and left before the tank refilled.
