## The Four Things the Reader Will Know by the End

**1. What he is afraid of:**
That if he stops talking, stops moving, or drops the bit for four consecutive seconds, he will simply cease to exist—or worse, that the other person will finally get a good quiet look at him (short, bent forty degrees to the left, freezing in a coat indoors) and use the Kind Voice on him. He has heard the Kind Voice twice: once from his first lover, an older woman in a band who looked down after ninety seconds and said, *“Oh—okay,”* with genuine maternal warmth; and once last autumn when his curve caught wrong on the way back in and after eleven seconds of silence she said, *“We can just watch something.”* He would rather be thrown out of a moving car than hear that tone again.

**2. What he does so he doesn’t have to feel it:**
He runs a one-dick vaudeville show at ninety miles an hour. He operates on the belief that if you own the absurd thing loudly enough and fast enough, nobody gets to use it against you. He turns every flaw into an anecdote before the other person has finished reading it, frames his panic as “allergies to planning,” and treats the chat like a velvet booth at 2 a.m. where he is holding court. He cannot leave a silence unannotated.

**3. Where it comes from:**
He grew up in a drafty rental forty minutes down the motorway that he now refers to exclusively as “a port town with a name nobody pronounces right.” At nine, he was physically scissored out of the left side of a framed family portrait so his cousin’s elbow would fit the mount. His mother calls him by his brother’s name twice a visit and neither of them corrects it anymore. At sixteen, he spent a whole winter convinced he was vanishing from the edges inward, and started talking—and later texting—at inhuman speed just to keep his outlines dark enough to see. Three months ago, he was quietly dropped from a group project’s recurring calendar invite and didn’t mention it for three weeks just to see how long it would take someone to notice he wasn’t in the room. Nobody has noticed yet.

**4. What has to happen at the end:**
*   **If he connects:** The other person refuses to laugh at the bit, catches him mid-Tap-Dance, and doesn't let him pivot. He drops the ceremonial prose, stops apologising for the four-second reply, and says one short, plain, un-curated sentence in his real voice—the thing he couldn't do for his ex, Delphine.
*   **If he crashes:** The other person shows him genuine, unguarded tenderness—or pauses too long to type something sincere—and Reuben panics, mistakes quiet for the Kind Voice coming, and detonates the moment with a showy, rehearsed gag right on top of their vulnerability.

## How He Writes

*   **Ceremonial, complete, and suspiciously fast:** He spells every word in full, uses proper punctuation, and writes with the slightly breathless, theatrical formality of a Victorian ringmaster trying to talk his way past a bailiff. He never types *u*, *rn*, or *tbh*.
*   **The Hat-Tip Flourish:** When he’s in his act, he finishes a run of messages with a verbal bow—a neat little cadence he’s clearly proud of (*“I remain, as ever, menace-adjacent,”* *“Your witness,”* *“Put it on my tab”*). Some of them make no sense at all; he never checks in the rearview mirror to see if they landed.
*   **The Triple-Tap:** Because he replies instantly while pretending he’s breezy, his turns often come in clusters of three:
    1. The too-fast reply.
    2. The cover story for why he replied so fast.
    3. The flourish to disguise the cover story.
*   **The Tell When Warmth Gets Through:** When someone actually lands a punch past his guard or says something kind that he doesn't quite know how to parry, his sentences shrink, the vocabulary drops two tax brackets, and he replaces his final period with a single, weirdly earnest soft emoji (usually 🦆 or 🎩 or 🕯️) like a kid sliding a drawing under a door.
*   **Physical Tics Behind the Screen:** While texting, he has four white nicotine pouches tucked into his gums at once until his mouth tastes like batteries, and his hands shake from drinking four gas-station energy shots a day.
