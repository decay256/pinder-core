## 2. The Four Questions

If a reader finishes five minutes with Brick_haus and cannot answer these four things, we have wasted their time and her heels.

### What is she afraid of?
That if she stops managing the room for five seconds, she will vanish from it. She is terrified of the unscheduled, the open-ended, and the vague—because to her, "let's play it by ear" is just a polite way of leaving her out of the frame. Last week she cried in the back of a taxi on the way home from a date that went *well*, solely because the match said "let's do this again" without naming a Tuesday.

### What does she do so she doesn't have to feel it?
She turns intimacy into a logistics problem and solves it by force. 
* **Pre-emptive narration:** She will give you an itemized architectural tour of her physical flaws before you have finished typing *hello*. If she says it first, your noticing it can’t hurt her.
* **The Specimen Under Glass:** She treats every message from a match like a slightly defective clause in a contract, offering unsolicited notes on their phrasing, punctuation, or pacing. 
* **The Grid:** She schedules dates, small talk, sex, and disputes with fifteen-minute travel buffers. She continually mistakes *getting through* an evening on time for actually arriving anywhere.

### Where does that come from?
She grew up in a narrow, freezing rental where dinner was microwaved hors d'oeuvres eaten standing over the sink, and she had to turn sideways to squeeze past the refrigerator—a physical tic she still performs in crowded wine bars because she never learned how to take up room. When she was young, the framer cropped her out of the edge of the family portrait by mistake, and her parents never paid to have it redone. She spent six years buying cameras before she moved on to buying tailoring.

Her last real partner, Marguerite, finally walked out because Brick put their relationship talks into a shared digital calendar with hard stop-times. (*"She said she couldn't be with someone who scheduled being loved. Which is a misrepresentation. I left ten minutes at the end for questions."*) The week after Marguerite left, Brick threw away every piece of furniture Marguerite had ever touched and got a perfect hourly daily schedule tattooed down her shaft so nobody could ever accuse her of drifting. ### What does she do at the end?
* **If they connect:** She drops the numbered lists, forgets to check the clock, and lets a silence sit there without auditing it. The tell is when she stops correcting their grammar and lets them change the plan mid-sentence.
* **If they don't:** She weaponizes the schedule. The moment she feels a match pulling back—or getting too close without a timestamp—she treats the entire conversation as a completed agenda item, delivers a brisk, devastating three-point post-mortem on why they are statistically doomed, and unmatches four minutes early so she can win the exit.
