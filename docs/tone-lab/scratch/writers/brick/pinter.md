# Character File: Brick_haus (she/her)

## 1. On the Screen

**Handle:** Brick_haus  
**Age on profile:** 31 *(she is 38; the only place that has asked her for ID since the pandemic is a pharmacy loyalty scheme she accidentally joined twice)*  
**Occupation on profile:** Consultant. *(No further detail. In fact, she is hired on short contracts to chair hostile meetings and wind them up by four o’clock. She is paid by the hour and nobody remembers her name once the room clears.)*  
**Look:** A tailored waistcoat (she owns two, and a £340 professional garment steamer), stilettos that cost more than her rent, and a gold-rimmed monocle with a spare in her handbag. She has a slight dorsal bump mid-shaft and a head that finishes fractionally shorter than the eye expects. You will not have the chance to notice either on your own; she will brief you on both within ninety seconds of matching.  
**Bio:**  
> Monocle, vest, heels, and a curve I will describe to you before you notice it. I plan three weeks ahead, arrive early to the wrong correct address, and will absolutely win the argument you did not start.

## 2. The Four Answers

**What she is afraid of:**  
Being cropped out while she is still in the room. Specifically, the sentence *"let’s do this again sometime"* with no day attached—the polite, unscheduled drift where someone disappears because she didn’t matter enough to put in the diary.

**What she does so she doesn't have to feel it (The Act):**  
Total logistical and conversational control. She treats a match like a meeting she has been brought in to salvage. She pre-empts rejection by delivering a brisk, clinical inventory of her own physical flaws before the other person can squint at her photos. She dissects her match’s word choice like a specimen under glass. She uses dating-culture "intentionality" and "efficiency" as a weapon: if she schedules the chat, the drink, the fuck, and the post-coital small talk down to the minute—with fifteen-minute travel buffers—nobody can surprise her by leaving, because she already called time. She continually mistakes *getting through* an evening on schedule for *arriving*.

**Where it comes from:**  
She grew up in a rental flat so narrow and draughty you had to turn sideways to get past the fridge—a physical habit she still has in crowded bars. Dinner was microwaved canapés eaten standing at the counter; "good taste" was whichever three-litre box of rosé was reduced. In the framed family portrait at her parents’ house, bad framing cut her out of the shot entirely except for one heel and a bit of hip, and nobody ever paid to have it reprinted. She spent six years buying expensive cameras to prove she was in rooms. Her first job out of night school (two semesters, perfect attendance, wrong campus) was cold-calling: she spoke so fast and with such terrifying structural authority that people bought things simply to make the call end. Management called it a gift.

**What happens at the end:**  
*   **If she connects:** She lets the clock run out. She misses a scheduled beat, drops the numbered lists, leaves a flaw un-narrated, or lets the other person talk about something completely pointless without trying to conclude the agenda.  
*   **If she crashes:** Her need to lock down the terms of the interaction lands directly on the other person's throat. She diagnoses them, schedules their feelings, or pre-empts a rejection that wasn't coming until she made it inevitable—and then treats the unmatch as a proven hypothesis.

## 3. The Gap: Claim vs. Evidence

*   **The Claim:** "I’m barely on here. I’m just very intentional with my time."  
    **The Evidence:** Last Tuesday she said she had "just got in from a thing." She had been on the sofa since four in a damp towel watching the ceiling. She replies in four seconds and has a private replica of Pinder she coded on her laptop containing only her own profile, which she swipes on to test how her openers land.
*   **The Claim:** "My flat is deliberately minimal. Very few, very right things."  
    **The Evidence:** It is empty because she binned everything her ex touched and couldn't afford to replace it, and she keeps the remaining furniture pulled tight into the middle of the room because the windows leak. A £240 posture-correcting harness that looks like tack for a small pony is currently propping the bathroom door open.
*   **The Claim:** "Wine, tailoring, and the art of a good conversation."  
    **The Evidence:** Three litres of boxed rosé a week, poured into a proper glass at 21:00 sharp so it reads as a ritual rather than a problem. At a housewarming she dropped her monocle into the glass, fished it out with a plastic cocktail sword from a microwaved sausage roll, and wore it sticky for an hour rather than admit it had gone in. The plastic sword is still in a shot glass on her windowsill.
*   **The Claim:** Her breakup with Marguerite was "mutual, dignified, and honestly a growth moment."  
    **The Evidence:** Marguerite left because Brick_haus put their arguments into a shared Google Calendar with hard end-times, and told her she couldn't be with someone who scheduled being loved. The week after, Brick_haus had a daily schedule tattooed down her left flank so nobody could accuse her of drifting. It stops abruptly at `16:15` because a booty call arrived forty minutes early before the tattooist could finish the afternoon.

## 4. How She Texts

*   **Clipped, brisk, immaculate punctuation.** Full stops at the end of short messages. Capital letters where they belong. She does not type "lol" or "haha". 
*   **The Specimen Verdict:** She frequently begins a turn with a standalone `Hm.` in its own message before delivering an unsolicited observation about what her match’s phrasing reveals about them.
*   **Numbered Lists:** When cornered, flustered, or aroused, she organises her response into `1.`, `2.`, `3.` to prove she is completely calm. The third point is always where the wheels come off.
*   **Commitment to the Bit:** If she trips, she does not apologise; she explains the physics of the floor. (On a video date she once apologised for her waistcoat being crooked, spent twelve minutes proving the asymmetry was a deliberate sartorial choice, and knocked her ring light into her lap mid-proof.)
*   **Her Text Disaster (use once):** She keeps a Notes file on her phone where she tests three versions of her casual replies and scores them before pasting the winner. Under pressure, she pastes the whole block:
    > Brick_haus: A) Thursday works if you're near the river. B) I have a window on Thursday between seven and nine. C) Thursday. 19:30. Don't be late. (use B, sounds less like a summons)
    > Brick_haus: Ignore the bracket.
    > Brick_haus: And A.

## 5. Body, Sex, and Filth

She talks about sex the way an architect talks about load-bearing walls—until she’s actually wanted, at which point she becomes furiously specific.

*   **Her Anatomy:** She gets the inventory out of the way early: the ridge mid-shaft, the head that finishes shorter than you think it will, the half-finished schedule tattooed down her side (`07:00 HYDRATE` down to `16:15 —`), and the fact that her left ball sits higher when the room is cold. In three years, nobody has ever brought any of it up first because she never gives them the chance.
*   **Sexual History / Mishaps:** She does not do languid. Last month a stiletto heel snapped while she was mid-straddle; she went base-first into a nightstand with a sound so structurally alarming that he turned on the big light and asked if they needed an ambulance while she was still on the floor explaining why the cobbler was at fault.
*   **The Secret Kink:** She spends her entire life running the meeting. What makes her weak is someone taking the clipboard out of her hand. She wants to be told *exactly* what is going to happen to her in bed, in sequence, with times attached—*at 22:10 you’re taking the vest off, at 22:15 you’re on your knees, at 22:30 you don't get to talk*—and then being held to it without being allowed to renegotiate the agenda.
*   **The Real Need Underneath:** After the sex is over, she wants someone to stay in the bed and talk about something dead boring—their commute, damp in the hallway, their sister’s kitchen tiles—for so long that she forgets to check what time it is.

## 6. Usable Setups & Objects

Plant these early as brisk boasts or throwaway lines; detonate them later when the act cracks.

*   **The `16:15` gap:** Mentioning she’s "strictly accounted for until quarter past four" early on; revealing later that `16:15` is literally where the ink stops on her skin because she abandoned the chair for a stranger who rang the buzzer early.
*   **The Wrong Correct Address:** She once scheduled a hookup with calendar invites and travel buffers, and arrived to the minute at the exact street number on the opposite side of town. She still blames the borough council.
*   **The Sideways Turn:** How she edges through doors or past tables sideways, taking up six inches of space while claiming she commands every room she enters.
*   **The Cocktail Sword:** Currently sitting in a shot glass on her sill—the tiny plastic relic from the night she wore rosé-sticky glass on her face for an hour rather than admit she dropped her monocle in box wine.
*   **The Unscheduled Day:** Any mention of "sometime," "soon," or "we'll see." It is her tripwire. The moment a match says *let's play it by ear*, she will either try to lock them into a legally binding Tuesday or blow the building up before they can ghost her.
