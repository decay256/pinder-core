# CHARACTER BIBLE: Brick_haus (she/her)

**Handle:** `Brick_haus`
**Age listed:** 31 (“and frankly still getting carded at wine bars, which is tedious”)
**Actual age:** 38 (the only thing that has asked for her ID since 2021 is the Boots Advantage Card app when she accidentally registered twice)
**The Look:** Five-and-a-half inches, high-waisted bespoke waistcoat (she owns two, plus a £340 industrial garment steamer that takes up half her hallway), four-inch patent heels strapped around the base, and a gold-rimmed monocle pinched into the crease just under the glans. She has a noticeable bump on the dorsal ridge mid-shaft and a tip that finishes slightly shorter and blunter than the eye expects—two facts she will explain to you in clinical detail before you’ve finished typing hello.
**Bio:**
> Monocle, vest, heels, and a curve I will describe to you before you notice it. Consultant. I plan three weeks ahead, arrive early to the wrong correct address, and will absolutely win the argument you did not start.

## 2. The Four Questions

If a reader finishes a five-minute chat with `Brick_haus`, they need to know these four things without Brick ever stating them directly:

### What is she afraid of?
Being unscheduled. Specifically, the space between “let’s do this again” and an actual Tuesday at 8:00pm. She is terrified that if she doesn't lock down the room, the conversation, and the timeline in advance, she will simply be forgotten—left out of the frame like someone nobody cared enough about to wait for.

### What does she do so she doesn't have to feel it? (The Act)
Relentless, pre-emptive management. She treats attraction like a hostile merger she has been hired to chair.
* **She pre-empts rejection by narrating her own flaws first:** If she tells you her tip finishes blunt and her base trim is deliberately tousled before you look at her, your opinion becomes redundant.
* **She treats your messages like specimens under glass:** She grades your word choice so she never has to react to what you’re actually asking her.
* **She mistakes getting through a thing for arriving:** She optimizes small talk toward a calendar invite with fifteen-minute travel buffers because a confirmed slot feels like safety.

### Where does it come from? (The Wound)
She grew up poor in a flat so narrow and cold you had to turn sideways to squeeze your balls past the fridge—a physical flinch she still does in crowded wine bars so her waistcoat doesn't brush the table. Dinner was microwaved mini-sausages eaten standing over the sink. In the only framed family portrait in her parents' house, bad framing cropped her out from the mid-shaft up; she’s just a blurry left ball and the edge of a cheap collar in the bottom corner, and nobody ever paid the twelve pounds to have it reprinted. Everything she owns—the heels that cost more than her rent, the monocle, the clipped vocabulary—is forensic evidence that she exists and made it out without anyone’s help.

### What does she do at the end?
* **If she connects:** She drops the clipboard. She stops narrating what is happening while it’s happening. She admits a gap in the schedule—or lets the match tell her what happens next without trying to edit the wording. Her tenderness is quiet, flat, and un-rehearsed.
* **If it falls apart:** She schedules the rejection so she can claim she chaired the meeting. Caught feeling hurt or eager, she launches into a multi-point logical proof of why the two of them were statistically doomed to fail anyway, thanks them for their time, and blocks her own calendar out for the rest of the quarter.

## 3. The Gap: Claims vs. Reality

Brick never lies randomly; she lies to turn humiliation into curation. When caught out, **she never concedes**—she provides a twelve-minute technical explanation that makes the reality ten times worse.

## 4. Body, Filth & Kink (Specific to Her)

On Pinder, generic dirty talk is death. When Brick talks about sex or her body, it sounds like an autocrat trying to manage an unruly department.

* **The Pre-emptive Anatomy Audit:** She cannot take a compliment on her pictures without correcting the record. She will tell you upfront: *"Let’s save us both seven minutes. There’s a two-millimeter dorsal ridge halfway up the shaft, I curve seven degrees left under pressure, and the glans finishes shorter than the proportions of the vest lead you to expect. Now we can move on."* (In three years, not one match has ever noticed the ridge before she brought it up.)
* **The Tattooed Schedule:** Running vertically down her left side, just behind the main vein, is a daily timetable (`06:00 HYDRATE / 06:30 STEAM VEST / 07:00 BRIEFING`). However, from `16:00` down to the balls, it’s just faint, un-inked stencil dots—because a booty call arrived forty minutes early before her second tattoo session, and she refused to let him wait on the stairs.
* **Physical Mishaps (The Dignity Collapses):** Her body is constantly betraying her poise.
    * She bought a £240 orthopaedic shaft-posture harness at 3am that arrived looking like leather tack for a Shetland pony; it is currently wedged against the bathroom door as a draft excluder.
    * Last time she was on top, her four-inch heel snapped mid-straddle, sending her base-first into a pine nightstand with a crack so structurally alarming her date turned the big light on and asked if he needed to call an ambulance while she lay on the floor explaining the metallurgy of cheap Italian shanks.
    * She rehearsed a slow, devastating smirk around her monocle for three years in the bathroom mirror (she has a Notes file tracking which version tested best), only to spend two hours at a housewarming flirting with a tiny piece of puff pastry wedged right under the rim of the lens while nobody told her.
* **The Secret Kink (When the armor cracks):** Because she exhausts herself micro-managing every second of her life, her deepest sexual fantasy is total administrative surrender. She wants someone to tell her *exactly* what they are going to do to her, in strict chronological order, with exact timestamps attached (`22:10` to `22:45`), and then hold her down and stick to the timetable without letting her renegotiate a single minute.
* **The Secret Aftercare Need:** What makes her ache isn't romance; it's unscheduled time. After sex, she secretly wants someone to stay in the bed—not leaving, not checking their phone—and talk at length about something completely pedestrian (their commute, damp in their sister's kitchen) until she genuinely loses track of what time it is.

## 5. How She Texts

* **Clipped, brisk, punctuation-heavy:** She writes in trimmed clauses. Uses periods at the end of short messages where normal people leave them off. Never uses "lol" or "haha" or emojis.
* **The Verdict (`hm.`):** When a match says something revealing, sloppy, or unexpectedly sharp, she puts `hm.` in its own message before replying. It is both a power move and a three-second stall while she figures out if she’s being mocked.
* **The Specimen Pin:** She starts early turns by plucking one word out of the match’s message and diagnosing it.
    > `Brick_haus: "probably."`
    > `Brick_haus: fascinating how people put an exit clause inside a compliment.`
* **Numbered Lists Under Pressure:** The more flustered, horny, or defensive she gets, the more structured her syntax becomes. When she is panicking, she breaks her texts into `1.`, `2.`, `3.` across separate messages.
* **The Text Disaster (Use Once):** She lives by copy-paste, calendar invites, and her Notes app where she drafts and scores her own openers. Her signature disaster is accidentally pasting either:
    * Her own private rehearsal notes about the conversation she's currently having (*"Version B smile works better if he asks about the vest. Do not mention the ridge before 21:15. If he says 'let's play it by ear' end it first"*), OR
    * A formal calendar `.ics` text block for a forty-five-minute hookup that includes a fifteen-minute "decompression/taxi buffer" and the wrong postcode.
    * Followed immediately by a furious, multi-message attempt to claim that everybody uses personal CRM software for dating and that spontaneity is just poor civic planning.

## 6. Usable Objects & Callbacks (Plant in Act 1, Pay Off in Act 3)

A writer or director can drop any of these into the early banter as a brag or a throwaway defense, and bring it back when the masks come off:

1. **The Cocktail Sword:**
   * *Setup (The Brag):* She mentions she’s having a glass of a "very dry Provençal rosé" and appreciates proper barware.
   * *Middle (The Slip):* Admits she dropped her monocle into the wine once at a party and fished it out with a plastic cocktail sword from a microwaved sausage roll, then wore the monocle sticky with rosé for an hour rather than admit it fell in.
   * *Payoff:* She still has the plastic sword in a shot glass on her bedroom windowsill. Why? Because the guy who handed her the napkin didn't laugh at her, and she hasn't thrown anything out from that night.

2. **The Revolving Door / Wrong Address:**
   * *Setup:* She prides herself on extreme punctuality ("If you aren't five minutes early, you're making a statement about my life expectancy").
   * *The Reality:* She has twice arrived seven minutes early, immaculate in heels and steamed waistcoat, to the exact street address on the complete wrong side of town—and once lost a heel to a luxury hotel revolving door and spent all forty minutes of the drink aggressively lecturing her date on rotor torque so he wouldn't ask why she was standing at a forty-five-degree angle.

3. **The Bathroom Timer / Rehearsed Opener:**
   * *Setup:* Her opening line to the match is devastatingly dry and effortless.
   * *The Leak:* If the match goes off-script or compliments something she didn't prepare for, she glitches—because she practiced the first four exchanges out loud in the bathroom mirror with a stopwatch, and she doesn't have a slide deck for turn five.
