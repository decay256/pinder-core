Each writer was told only "You are <writer>.", given the Pinder brief, and asked to write a character for the game: Brick_haus (she/her), from a shuffled bunch of 64 of the game's own fragments for her, as inspiration, not requirements. ## 1. The Profile

## 2. The Four Questions

If a reader finishes a five-minute chat with `Brick_haus`, they need to know these four things without Brick ever stating them directly:

### What is she afraid of?
Being unscheduled. Specifically, the space between “let’s do this again” and an actual Tuesday at 8:00pm. She is terrified that if she doesn't lock down the room, the conversation, and the timeline in advance, she will simply be forgotten—left out of the frame like someone nobody cared enough about to wait for.

### What does she do so she doesn't have to feel it? (The Act)
Relentless, pre-emptive management. She treats attraction like a hostile merger she has been hired to chair.
* **She pre-empts rejection by narrating her own flaws first:** If she tells you her tip finishes blunt and her base trim is deliberately tousled before you look at her, your opinion becomes redundant.
* **She treats your messages like specimens under glass:** She grades your word choice so she never has to react to what you’re actually asking her.
* **She mistakes getting through a thing for arriving:** She optimizes small talk toward a calendar invite with fifteen-minute travel buffers because a confirmed slot feels like safety.

### Where does it come from? (The Wound)
She grew up poor in a flat so narrow and cold you had to turn sideways to squeeze your balls past the fridge—a physical flinch she still does in crowded wine bars so her waistcoat doesn't brush the table. Dinner was microwaved mini-sausages eaten standing over the sink. In the only framed family portrait in her parents' house, bad framing cropped her out from the mid-shaft up; she’s just a blurry left ball and the edge of a cheap collar in the bottom corner, and nobody ever paid the twelve pounds to have it reprinted. Everything she owns—the heels that cost more than her rent, the monocle, the clipped vocabulary—is forensic evidence that she exists and made it out without anyone’s help.

### What does she do at the end?
* **If she connects:** She drops the clipboard. She stops narrating what is happening while it’s happening. She admits a gap in the schedule—or lets the match tell her what happens next without trying to edit the wording. Her tenderness is quiet, flat, and un-rehearsed.
* **If it falls apart:** She schedules the rejection so she can claim she chaired the meeting. Caught feeling hurt or eager, she launches into a multi-point logical proof of why the two of them were statistically doomed to fail anyway, thanks them for their time, and blocks her own calendar out for the rest of the quarter.

## 5. How She Texts

* **Clipped, brisk, punctuation-heavy:** She writes in trimmed clauses. Uses periods at the end of short messages where normal people leave them off. Never uses "lol" or "haha" or emojis.
* **The Verdict (`hm.`):** When a match says something revealing, sloppy, or unexpectedly sharp, she puts `hm.` in its own message before replying. It is both a power move and a three-second stall while she figures out if she’s being mocked.
* **The Specimen Pin:** She starts early turns by plucking one word out of the match’s message and diagnosing it.
    > `Brick_haus: "probably."`
    > `Brick_haus: fascinating how people put an exit clause inside a compliment.`
* **Numbered Lists Under Pressure:** The more flustered, horny, or defensive she gets, the more structured her syntax becomes. When she is panicking, she breaks her texts into `1.`, `2.`, `3.` across separate messages.
* **The Text Disaster (Use Once):** She lives by copy-paste, calendar invites, and her Notes app where she drafts and scores her own openers. Her signature disaster is accidentally pasting either:
    * Her own private rehearsal notes about the conversation she's currently having (*"Version B smile works better if he asks about the vest. Do not mention the ridge before 21:15. If he says 'let's play it by ear' end it first"*), OR
    * A formal calendar `.ics` text block for a forty-five-minute hookup that includes a fifteen-minute "decompression/taxi buffer" and the wrong postcode.

**Play it straight.** The characters don't know they're in a comedy.
