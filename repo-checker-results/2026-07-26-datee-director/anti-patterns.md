### Finding 1: Director validator uses broad lexical traps for semantic intent
**File**: `src/Pinder.LlmAdapters/EmotionalDirectorContract.cs:293`
**Issue**: `ContainsDraftedReply()` rejects any first-person marker or any double quote in every director field (`return FirstPersonMarkerRegex.IsMatch(value) || value.IndexOf('"') >= 0;`), and `ContainsMetaLanguage()` rejects unbounded substrings such as `prompt`, `director`, `schema`, and `json` via `lower.Contains(...)`. This conflates ordinary private-reaction prose with drafted chat/meta output; for example, a private emotional field phrased as "I feel cautious but curious" trips `drafted_chat_reply`, and "responds promptly but guardedly" trips `meta_language` because it contains `prompt`.
**Impact**: Legitimate model outputs can be rejected and retried until the emotional-director operation fails once it is wired into the DATEE response flow. The current tests lock in rejection of clear direct speech, but they do not cover benign first-person private analysis or accidental substring matches.
**Urgency**: U3 - topic default for a style/heuristic smell; kept at U3 because the operation is currently private and not invoked from the DATEE response path, but it should be cleaned before wiring.
**Fixer-Agent Action Plan**: Replace the all-fields lexical traps with field-aware validation: only reject quoted/full-sentence drafted replies where a DATEE response field is expected, use word-boundary or phrase-level meta-language checks, and add regressions that accept private first-person/emotion-analysis prose while still rejecting actual drafted chat text and explicit prompt/schema leakage.
