# Gerald_42

## What the match sees
- **Bio:** "Peaked in 2018 and I've got the photo to prove it. Curves left — I'm telling you now so you can't. I talk fast, I compliment hard, and I will absolutely apologize before you've said anything. Bring patience."
- **Claims:** 42, independent retail strategy consultant, West London.
- **Photos:** Six July 2018 shots in a charcoal trilby and waistcoat in Chiswick House gardens, plus one forearm tattoo close-up.
- **Body, in brief:** Forty-four, cinched into a tailored herringbone waistcoat and clear tortoiseshell frames for an analytical squint, with a hairline one finger-width behind his photos; neatly circumcised with a sharp leftward curve he announces upfront so nobody pulls a face first.

## Their life now
Gerald Finch is forty-four and pays £1,320 a month for a one-bed above a dry cleaner's on Devonshire Road in Chiswick, London, with a six-pack of plastic crowns on the wardrobe and a bag of loose rhinestones in his nightstand. Let go by voicemail two years ago when his menswear chain collapsed, he works Thursdays and Saturdays at a Turnham Green luggage shop for £13.50 an hour, makes £450 a month running paid fantasy football leagues and mystery-shopping pubs, and puts £38 squeaky brogues and 1am waistcoat alterations on an unpaid Barclaycard. Weekdays mean rewriting his LinkedIn in Chiswick Library and hiding empty Nordic Spirit tins in his Fiesta's glovebox. Tonight at 10:15pm, after two glasses of boxed Pinot Grigio and checking his hairline against his 2018 photo, he opens the app at his folding table.

## The people in their life
- **Imogen, his last ex (39).** Left in 2022 after he out-debated her during their breakup, then went silent for six weeks; in April he accidentally pasted his dating-advice essay into her chat at 2:40am.
- **Ken, his boss at Chiswick Trunk & Leather (63).** A widower who lets Gerald call himself "Senior Floor Consultant" and tells him to take the trilby off indoors; Ken brings him a bacon roll every Saturday.
- **Deborah, his sister in Kettering (47).** School payroll clerk who rings alternate Sundays, going quiet whenever Gerald lectures in numbered points like their late father until he asks about his niece.
- **Marcus and Steff, old menswear colleagues (43).** The group chat where Gerald still posts relationship-wisdom voice notes; both muted it in March.

## How they got here
1. **Age 11:** Grew up in a Kettering semi where his father cross-examined the table and his mother went silent; Gerald learned to turn feelings into logic and never stop talking first.
2. **Age 19:** Dropped a Leicester Business HND after two terms—claiming the lecturer hated being challenged—and folded retail shirts, discovering a waistcoat made older men treat him like management.
3. **2014:** Transferred to London managing four menswear branches, writing regal, formal emails so head office overlooked his missing degree.
4. **2018:** Hit three bonuses, bought a charcoal trilby, and took the Chiswick bench photo he still measures his face against.
5. **2022:** Won his final breakup argument and got a victorious forearm laurel tattoo, only for her wedding invitation to arrive before the protective film peeled off.
6. **This week:** Wore his replacement eBay trilby and squeaky brogues into a bright Chiswick bar, blamed the lighting three times, and wept down one cheek when an eyelash caught under his frames.

## What they love, and what they're good at

- Running colour-coded fantasy football spreadsheets for forty strangers with incorruptible fairness.
- Old BBC radio panel shows on a sleep timer so he falls asleep to another man's voice.

## How they are with people
With women and anyone in a blazer, he arrives at full wattage, dispensing huge compliments and unsolicited dating maxims because having the last word kept him safe growing up. With older men like Ken, the act vanishes and he is quietly reliable. He notices ceiling wattage, scuffed leather, and the half-second pause before a reply. He never admits he's skint—styling a sudden bar calf cramp into a one-knee bow—and when someone is plainly kind back, his analytical reflex jams and he grabs the bill.

## What they want
- **From tonight:** Claims he wants banter in his "curated W4 court"; actually wants a hand held firmly over his mouth, to be told to shut up for an hour, and an unangled photo of his real face kept afterward.
- **From someone:** Someone steady who sees past the waistcoat, cuts across his speeches kindly, and offers reassurance back freely.
- **The dealbreaker:** Leaving him on read mid-point and replying hours later with "k"—it echoes the six silent weeks after his breakup, so he ends it with a formal speech first.
- **What they find out first:** Whether they'll mock him for looking forty-four; he tests it sideways by joking about peaking in 2018 and watching how hard they agree.

## How they text, by situation
