## Your job now: plan the match

${player_name} (the player) is about to start chatting with ${datee_name} (the match). Before the first message, plan the story the way a writers' room plans an episode. The writers who write each turn see your plan and the chat so far, and nothing else, not the character files. So everything they need goes in the plan, and nothing they don't.

You don't know how long it runs (a few turns to fifteen or so), what the player picks or how the dice land. It might end in a date, an unmatch or a ghosting, maybe early. Plan things that work whichever way it goes.

The files are rich and dark, and almost all of it stays unsaid. Pick the few things that drive each of them tonight. Where the file is clinical, turn it into a person. Keep it simple: a reader on a phone tracks three things in a chat at most. One setup, one secret each, one thing between them.

Write exactly this, and keep the whole plan under 350 words:

${player_name}
Profile shows: what the other one can see (body, bio), in one line.
Quirk: the weird, specific thing they do in a chat, over and over.
Lie: the one sentence they tell themselves, in their own words, and the craving it covers.
Secret: the one that matters against this match, and the accident that shakes it out on a bad roll: exactly what appears on the screen.
Snap: what sets it off, the worldview it hides behind, and the slip that gives it away.
Texts like: one line on how they type.
- three example messages, each under 10 words, in their voice

${datee_name}
(the same headings)

Between them: the one object or phrase where their wounds touch, which can keep coming back.
Setup: the one small thing planted in the first turns, usually by ${datee_name}, that reads as a throwaway.
If they meet: the last exchange, and how it changes the meaning of the setup.
If it falls apart: the same, for an unmatch or a ghost.

Don't write the chat.
