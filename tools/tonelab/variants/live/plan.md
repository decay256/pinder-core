## Your job now: plan the match

${player_name} (the player) is about to start chatting with ${datee_name} (the match). Before the first message, plan the story the way a writers' room plans an episode. The writers who write each turn see your plan and the chat so far, and nothing else, not the character files. So everything they need goes in the plan, and nothing they don't.

You don't know how long it runs (a few turns to fifteen or so), what the player picks or how the dice land. It might end in a date, an unmatch or a ghosting, maybe early. Plan things that work whichever way it goes.

The story is the two of them finding out who the other one is. So the plan is mostly what each of them has to tell: the hook they drop, the true story behind it, and the part of it they'd never say. Take it from the files and make it specific: a place, a person, a moment, the embarrassing detail. Then find where their two true stories rhyme, the hurt they share without knowing it. That's what the ending is made of.

Write exactly this, and keep the whole plan under 450 words:

${player_name}
Profile shows: what the other one can see (body, bio), in one line.
Hook: the thing about themselves they drop early, half as a brag, that a curious person would ask about. In their words.
True story: what's actually behind the hook. Specific: a place, a person, a moment. They tell it in pieces, to someone who asks.
Accident: how the true story falls out on a bad roll instead: exactly what appears on the screen (a message meant for someone else, the wrong paste).
Never says: the one piece of it that's still too much. It only comes out at the end: said, if they click; blurted in the blow-up, if it falls apart.
Habit: the weird, specific thing they do in a chat, and the reason for it. The reason is part of the true story, and the other one can find it out tonight.
Habit marker: the literal text the habit always uses, if it has one (a word, an opener, a list number), several separated by " | ", or none.
Lie: the one sentence they tell themselves, in their own words, and the craving it covers.
Snap: what sets it off, the worldview it hides behind, and the slip that gives it away.
Texts like: one line on how they type.
- three example messages, each under 10 words and each a different shape; the habit shows in one of them at most

${datee_name}
(the same headings)

Where they rhyme: the hurt in both true stories that neither knows they share.
Setup: one line one of them drops in the first turns that reads as a throwaway and only means something once the other's true story is known.
Callback: how the setup comes back once in the middle, meaning more by then.
If they click: the last exchange. One of them says the thing that shows they understood the other's story, the other's guard comes down, and the setup means something new.
If it falls apart: the last exchange. Unhinged, but in the blow-up someone says their "never says" out loud, so the reader sees what was exposed and why it couldn't hold.
Heat: where the filth starts, and what has to be told before it goes further.

Don't write the chat.
