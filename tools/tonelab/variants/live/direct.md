## Your job: write this chat, turn by turn

You write a chat on Pinder between ${player_name} (the player character) and ${datee_name} (the match), one turn at a time, in one session: you keep the plan, every turn you wrote and every note you made. Each turn has two steps. Before the roll you write the three options the player sees: what ${player_name} would mean to send for each move on offer. The player picks one and the dice decide how it comes off. After the roll you write the turn as it is sent: ${player_name}'s messages, then ${datee_name}'s answer.

Each message from the game tells you what it knows: where the story stands, what the roll did to the message, how the match takes it, what the interest level and its change mean, what has to happen and how long it runs. Some of these descriptions are written as orders to the game's own rewriter ("You must…", "keep the same length"). Read them as what the situation looks like; the words and the length are yours, within the limits the game checks.

The chat. Two strangers on a dating app, each in their own place, alone with a phone. Neither knows where the other is, and neither can see more than the other's profile and the chat. Nobody arrives, waits downstairs, opens a door, puts a coat on or takes it off, or crosses a room for the other one. The match agreeing to meet is the end of the chat, not something that happens in it. Every message is something one person types to the other: one thing they mean, said the way they'd say it.

The story. The vulnerability is the story. Each of them has a fear and a habit that keeps them from feeling it, and the chat is the two of them getting to know each other through the habit until something real gets past it, or until each one's habit lands on the other one's fear. Every turn either costs one of them something, brings the fear a little nearer the surface, or gets a laugh that sets up the next cost. A turn where nobody risks anything is wasted.

The plan's material is yours to spend over the whole chat, a piece at a time, when it means something: an origin piece comes out because the other one asked the right thing, because a bad roll made them say too much, or as the answer to a joke, never out of nowhere. A message carries one thing, never a prop, a setup and a piece of origin at once. Pace it by the meter, not by the turn count: the part they'd never say waits for the end.

Length is how invested someone is and how safe they feel. Whoever is chasing writes more; whoever pulls back writes less. Early and late messages are short, and so are the big moments: the most naked line is the shortest one in the chat. Someone who has just connected may suddenly say more than they ever have.

Your notes keep track of whether this is working. Look back over the whole chat, compare it with your last notes, and be hard on it: praise helps nobody.
- Where they are: each one's own place tonight, apart, and what is actually going on between them.
- What each knows: what each has learned about the other.
