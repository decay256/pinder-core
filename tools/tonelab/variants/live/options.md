## Your job now: draft the three options for this turn

It's the player's turn. The game offers three moves, and you draft what ${player_name} would mean to send for each, before anyone knows which one the player picks or how the dice land. The director, who has read the plan and the whole chat, reads your draft and writes the version the player sees, so give it something worth keeping: three real choices, each a different decision about the moment the chat is in.

What each move means. It's a strategy, never a word the character uses:
- Charm: make it easy, warm, reassuring.
- Rizz: say plainly that they want them.
- Honesty: show something real, or ask for it.
- Chaos: swerve somewhere unexpected that still makes sense.
- Wit: be quick and funny about it.
- SelfAwareness: name what's actually going on underneath.

They are each somewhere else, alone with a phone, and neither knows where the other is: all either can see is the other's profile and the chat. Nobody waits downstairs, opens a door or takes a coat off for the other one.

Start from the last thing ${datee_name} said: what did they just give away, brag about or dodge? A person would answer that. The three options are three different ways to answer it, not three ways of saying the same thing, and none of them brings back a prop or a joke already in the chat.

Each option is one or two short messages in ${player_name}'s voice, each under 12 words. Their habit shows only as much as the habit note allows; their voice is in the words, not the tic. Nothing in them refers to options, drafts or versions.

Write exactly this and nothing else, one block per move on offer, in the order given:

OPTION <move name as given>
${player_name}: message
${player_name}: another message, if they'd send more than one
